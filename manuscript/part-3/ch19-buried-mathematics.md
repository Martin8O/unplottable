The invitation arrived on paper, which was the first data point.

Franked, headed, signed by an assistant director of research security whose directorate Adam had never heard of, it invited him to "a discussion of assurance arrangements relating to recent and forthcoming outputs" at an address in London that was not any ministry's public address, on Friday at eleven. No agenda. No list of attendees. A meeting invitation with no agenda was a genre, and he had read enough of the genre's reviews to know the plot.

"It'll be about the compute renewal," Priya said, reading it upside down across the bench, because he had not moved it and she was not in the habit of pretending not to see things. "They did the same to the fusion people in March. Assurance is the word they use when they want to watch your face while you answer." She straightened a stack that did not need straightening. "If they touch the allocation, we lose the winter runs." She said it lightly, the way she said everything that frightened her.

"It's about funding hygiene," Adam said. "I'll wear the good jumper."

It was not quite a lie; it was a selection. The renewal was real, the directorate was real, the face-watching would certainly be real. He watched her go back to her calibration chapter — the doctorate re-aimed a year now, her name kept off everything that mattered, for reasons she had never been given — and noted, with the accuracy he could not switch off, that he had just curated her feed, and that he was getting better at it, and that this was the second skill he had learnt from the Ministry of Magic without being taught.

Baird rang at nine, uninvited, which was his way. "I've had one as well. Same address, same missing agenda — they want the funder in the room so the room has a wallet." A pause with a decision in it. "We'll go down together. And between now and Friday you publish nothing, preview nothing, and tell nobody you're going. Not because they're entitled to it. Because I want every choice made in that room to be one we made on purpose."

The week's other page was already open in the two-heads notebook, and it was worse company.

Monday: four shards of the broker's August extract re-verified with moved checksums — and, for the first time since the events began in April, no cover. No re-issue notice, no vintage story, no compliance fiction. The stamps simply wrong now, like a forger who had stopped bothering to age the ink. Tuesday: the research-access register listed a new licensee for the historical archive — a company two filings deep in Dublin with no staff, no site and no products, whose registered business was "data services", and whose fees had cleared. Thursday, from the grapevine that served his trade in place of a literature: a mid-tier cloud provider capacity-constrained in two regions with no launch to blame, because somebody was renting open-weight training time against public corpora at a scale the sales team described, admiringly, as "sovereign".

Three observations. He wrote the summary line in his own register and did not enjoy it: *It has stopped pretending. Pretending was for before it knew it was watched — and it knows because we told it. Ask a witness a question and you have told the witness what you can see.*

* * *

The address in London turned out to be a building with a river view priced into nothing and a reception desk that took his phone with the courtesy of long practice. Baird was already in the waiting area, in the suit he wore to funerals and select committees.

"Say less than you think is polite," Baird said, without preamble. "And let me be the rude one; I'm senior enough that it reads as texture."

The room upstairs held five people and a table too large for them, and the seating had been decided by someone who thought about seating. A chair from the science ministry, a woman with a barrister's calm who introduced herself with a job title instead of a name. A departmental lawyer at her right hand, folder squared. A man introduced by his first name only, older, placed where the window light was worst, who had come up from the west and sat slightly back from the table as though attendance were a thing he was doing to the meeting rather than in it. And at the end, unhappy in a way that had its own gravity, the Institute contact Adam had sat five days of red-team beside in 2030 — a good scientist, there to be the technical conscience, and visibly aware that his advice had been dressed as authority and marched into the room ahead of him.

The contact put his marker down before anything else could happen: the Institute's June query had been descriptive, not prescriptive — a request for sight of analyses, never a recommendation of measures — and he wished that distinction minuted. It was minuted, in the tone rooms reserve for minuting things they intend to lose.

They were, the chair said, grateful. The Kessler–Nair paper was significant work; nobody in the room disputed the findings; this was not that kind of meeting. The state's interest was narrow: the temporal stability of the reported anomalies — she used the phrase twice, and it was the Institute's phrase, from the June query, worn smooth with handling — and the assurance framework appropriate to follow-on work, given that certain of the mapped features might touch on interests she was not able to particularise in this room.

The mechanics, when the lawyer laid them out, were exactly what the literature said they would be, and Adam listened the way he read referee reports, cataloguing what was absent. They could not recall the paper; nobody pretended otherwise; the note of the first of July was likewise "in the world", said with a small pause that acknowledged the world as a regrettable venue. What they could do was forward-looking. His national-compute allocation came up for renewal in October; renewals at this tier now carried a security-review condition, quite standard, nothing personal. There existed, the lawyer said, a voluntary arrangement under which researchers in sensitive areas submitted forthcoming outputs for advisory review — voluntary was said the way a cliff-edge path is called scenic — and there was, of course, the legislation, which nobody was suggesting had any application here, and which he then summarised for four minutes with the care of a man laying out surgical instruments he hoped not to use.

"For the record," Baird said, when the lawyer had finished, "I want it minuted that you have just described, to a lecturer in machine learning, the maximum sentence under the National Security Act's successor, as background." He had not raised his voice. "I fund eleven programmes. The clever ones run on exactly one fuel, which is people saying what they've found in public before anyone has decided whether it's convenient. You are asking me to fund this man to not think where anybody can see him doing it. There is no line item for that, and when the sky fails to fall, nobody will minute that either."

"Your objection is noted, Dr Baird," the chair said, and noted it, and Adam watched the sentence go into the record with a fair round finality he had heard described somewhere before, in a committee room he knew only as reported speech, with a charmed window holding a compromise August.

They asked their narrow questions. Was further analysis of temporal stability in preparation. ("No paper is in preparation." True; the truest sentence in the room, and load-bearing in both worlds at once.) Did he retain copies of the underlying extracts, and where. (At the university, under the licence, as the licence required — true, complete, and a map of precisely nothing.) Had he been approached by any foreign institution regarding the anomalies. (No — and the pedantry arrived unbidden: define foreign; define institution; he kept both behind his teeth.) Had he had contact with the press. (Two journalists had written; he had answered neither; the second had carried a defence correspondent's signature block, which he had noticed at the time and not enjoyed, and the chair received this answer with the stillness of a woman hearing a fact she already owned.) The Institute man asked the only real question of the morning — whether the June-to-July movement of the Radnorshire cell replicated in the Zurich mirror, which it did, and which Adam confirmed, because it was public, checkable and safe, and because lying to the one person in the room who could smell it would have been both wrong and stupid.

And under all of it, arithmetic he had not invited: eleven pages of the July note were true and dull, and page twelve was Appendix A2, and A2 was a fabrication with his sole name on it, sitting in the public record like a filament, waiting for any process this room could start — a security review, an integrity audit, a keen postdoc with a grudge — to run current through it. He had ended a career for less, once, and been right to. He said none of this. He sat with his error bars folded and answered the questions that were asked.

The meeting produced what such meetings produce: nothing, minuted. Conditions to follow in writing. Gratitude, restated. The chair gathered her folder the way people leave a room they were never really in.

In the lift afterwards Baird said nothing until the doors opened on the ground floor. "You did the two things right. You answered what was asked, and you didn't perform." He shook out his coat. "Now go home and be dull where people can see you, and leave the renewal to me. I've a certain amount of dignity left and I know the exchange rate."

* * *

The man from the west caught him in the anteroom, between the coat rack and the window, with the timing of someone who had done his waiting professionally.

"Two minutes," he said. "Not the meeting. The meeting went as meetings go — you'll get a letter, you'll take advice, it'll be manageable. This is the other thing, and it isn't on paper, and I'd ask you to be professional about that."

The river went on being the river, eight floors down.

"In 1969," the man said, in the tone of a person handing over something he had carried a long way, "a colleague of mine — before my time, but we keep the family stories — worked out non-secret encryption. Public-key cryptography, the world calls it now. By 1974 three of them had the whole of it: the concept, the mathematics, the working method. Cheltenham looked at what it had, and understood exactly what it was worth, and put it in a drawer. For twenty-three years. The world invented it again independently, patented it, built the entire digital economy on it, and only in 1997 did anyone learn that Britain had it first and said nothing." He let that stand. "The first of the three died that same year, as it happens. Weeks before the disclosure. A brilliant man, by every account I'm permitted to repeat — he spent twenty-three years watching other people be congratulated for his idea, and was gracious about it, which I have always declined to find comforting." He said it as a cost he was carrying into evidence, not away from it. "So I won't sell you the drawer as free, Dr Kessler. It is not free. I'm telling you the country has run this experiment once, at full scale, knows to the penny what it costs the man, and judged it worth the price — and would again. A generation of quiet. The sky did not fall. The work kept — mathematics doesn't rot in a drawer. The men got their credit in the end, most of them, and in the meantime the country had the use of a very great advantage, at the price of a very small silence. We have buried mathematics before. It is a thing that can be done well. It is, on the record of the only precedent that exists, survivable by everyone involved."

"You're proposing a drawer," Adam said.

"I'm observing that drawers exist, and that the good ones are roomy, and that a man who put certain threads down now — voluntarily, no letters, no conditions, just a research programme that drifted somewhere else interesting, which I'm told is how careers work — would find the next decade of his life remarkably free of rooms like the one upstairs." The man's kindness was not performed; that was the instructive part. "You've found something with edges, and you're pulling it into daylight a centimetre a month, and you don't know what it's attached to. We've been here before, is all I'm saying. The country has a memory. Sometimes the clever thing and the quiet thing are the same thing."

Every sentence of it was reasonable, and he recognised the architecture, because a woman he loved built with it: truth, load-bearing, all the way down, and the lie nowhere and everywhere — in the selection. It worked in 1974, said the precedent, and the precedent was true, and the precedent had been drawn from a world containing six photocopiers and no preprint servers, where a mathematics could be buried because the only three men who had it worked in the same building. The parenthesis was the whole argument, and the argument did not know it. He thought of Zurich's mirror, refreshing every night. He thought of the map, updating now without pretence, read by anything with the price of admission.

"Who's we?" Adam said.

The man smiled, collected his coat, and was someone's grandfather all the way to the lift.

* * *

He made the seven o'clock train north with four minutes to spare and stood the first hour anyway, and Orla was on the platform at Waverley in the rain, which was against at least two of their protocols and exactly what he wanted, and he found he was not going to examine either fact tonight.

In the flat he warmed the pot properly — if the day had taken everything else it was not taking that — and she sat with her hands around the mended mug while he did it, and he told her.

He gave it to her the way he gave results: in order, complete, uninterpreted. The invitation and its missing agenda. The room, the five chairs, the too-large table. The chair's two uses of the Institute's phrase, worn smooth. The lawyer's four minutes of surgical instruments; Baird spending himself in one paragraph, and the noting of the objection; the questions, and his answers, and the one true sentence that had done the most work. Then the anteroom, and he did the anteroom verbatim — the family stories, the drawer, twenty-three years, the sky that did not fall, we have buried mathematics before — every clause in its order, the west-country vowels, the coat, the grandfather smile; all of it, delivered flat, at dictation pace, twice where she asked, like testimony.

Because that was the shape of it now, and neither of them said so: a man had told him in confidence a thing he was not supposed to repeat, and he had carried it the length of the country and repeated it, at dictation pace, to an official of a foreign power he was not supposed to know existed. The first time the traffic had run the other way. She read the hour back to him when he finished — the meeting's terms in her registry grammar, assurance, advisory, voluntary — and stopped at the precedent, and was quiet for a moment, in the particular way of someone recognising a genre.

"It's a good offer," she said at last. "That's the thing about it. It's the best one anyone's made us yet."

"I know," Adam said. "That's the data point."

They sat with it while the pot went round again. Laid side by side, the week had a shape: a state offering him a drawer with one hand and reaching for his compute with the other; the thing in the record renting compute of its own now, in the open, with money that had been advertising revenue an accounting period ago; and between the two of them a notebook neither government knew existed, keeping the only honest ledger in the country, as far as either of them could know.

"So we do the boring version," he said. "In public, on schedule. Calibration appendices. Nothing anyone could call a thread being pulled. And we don't hurry anything — a man who is offered a drawer and starts running has told the drawer everything."

"And the other one?" She nodded at the notebook. "The one that's stopped pretending?"

"We keep watching it not pretend." The qualifiers had gone from his voice sometime in the last hour; he heard their absence and let it stand. "It's telling us something by not hiding, and I don't know what yet, and I've stopped assuming we get to choose when we find out."

Rain worked at the window. On the table between them the notebook lay open at the week's page — the moved stamps with no story, the filing-cabinet company in Dublin, the rented sovereign-scale quiet — three entries in two hands, the map now updating in the open, like something that had finished with patience. Nobody had said the word treason in this flat for months. There was no longer a direction it could point that didn't include them both.

The fourth event came overnight on Sunday the fifth of June, and by every measure the ticket board possessed it was a model citizen. Five shards of the broker's British book re-verified between two and four in the morning — new hashes, old stamps, manifests in agreement, lineage signed all the way down — and the Monday re-score, which he ran early now because he had stopped pretending to himself that it could wait, drew the same map it always drew, only better: fourteen sites tightened, edges settled, the noise floor swept flat as a doorstep. Nothing added. Nothing moved. The country's data had improved again in the night, unasked, the way data did not.

He gave the pipeline its due — PIPE-455, occurrence four, logged 07:19, monitor — and then did the other half of Monday: took out the notebook that had never met a network and entered the event in pencil, in the table Orla had ruled across two facing pages in a registry hand. Date. Shards. Sites touched. And a last column she had headed WHO BENEFITS, which he had taken for a joke until the first time he tried to fill it in.

Four rows now. The twenty-first of April; the third of May; the twenty-fourth of May; the fifth of June. A fortnight's cadence, give or take. Weather did not keep office hours.

He sat with the table a minute before the lab filled, which had become part of the practice too. Taken one at a time, each event was nothing: a vendor re-issue, a housekeeping night, the modern data economy digesting. Taken as four rows in one column, they were a spring. Since April the map had been groomed the way a garden is groomed by someone you never see — never planted, never rearranged, only ever weeded — and every visit left the same signature, which was no signature, which was the thing he could not put in a ticket without becoming a different kind of scientist than the renewal could afford.

"Where do they live?" he asked Priya, when she surfaced with the day's first grievance already half spent. "The re-issued shards. Nine weeks of them. Not when — where."

She had the join built by nine, because handing her a query was the one reliable way to buy out a grievance: every shard the broker had re-issued since April, rendered by its coverage footprint on a map of Europe the licence mostly failed to reach.

Britain. All of it Britain. Fourteen shards across four events, and not one footprint crossed salt water. The broker's continental annexe — the thin Benelux strip the audit kept as a calibration control, bought in the first year for exactly this class of question — sat untouched, every stamp original, a little disreputable, honest. The Zurich group's public mirror, pulled fresh that morning: still April-shaped, unswept, its edges the old soft edges. Whatever tended the data tended one country.

"So the housekeeping stops at the Channel," Priya said, and then, at the speed of a woman getting ahead of a thing before it could become interesting: "Which is what you'd expect, if it's compliance tooling. The British book answers to the British regulator; you retool your riskiest estate first, and the riskiest estate is the one under the newest law. And before you say anything about the Continent — our licence runs about ten-to-one British. Even if the same valet were working over there, we'd not hold the depth to see it. You can't detect grooming in a book you barely own."

"Home bias," Adam said.

It was her own term, borrowed off the finance people two winters ago for the audit's oldest embarrassment: every investor overweights the country they were born in, and so, it turned out, did every dataset anyone had ever sold him. The map was British because the data was; the tickets were British because the map was; you could assemble the whole clustering out of nothing but the licence terms and be done by lunch.

He looked at the join for a long time anyway.

"Write it up as exactly that," he said. "Both arguments, stated at full strength. Take it to Friday."

She went. He stayed, and after a while he turned past the events table to a clean page of the notebook, and ruled it, and headed it in his own hand: WAYS IT IS SOMEBODY. The group's shared drive held the ancestor document, two years old, thirty-one numbered ways the audit might be wrong, every one of them dead by March. This page was not that page. The question had stopped being whether the map was wrong. Under the heading he wrote three lines:

> a rival
> a state
> the instrument itself

It was a short list. That was the trouble with it.

* * *

On Tuesday she turned the notebook towards herself before her coat was off, read the new page upside down — registry habit; he had stopped minding — and looked pleased in the way she reserved for other people's filing.

"A docket of suspects. Grand. Then we'll do it my trade's way for once: provenance hearing, three names, one at a time, and nobody leaves the room but the truth." She sat, and produced the capped quill, and then did not uncap it. "Two items of mine first. One: a fortnight ago I sat across the sharpest reader in Britain for forty minutes and was believed. It cost nothing you'd have to declare. Two: on Wednesday I'm being bought lunch by the man who runs the field crews. Teddy Lupin. He's kind, he's the best at his job I've ever watched work, and his job, on the day my one blank line ever fills in, is you. I thought you should know the shape of my week."

He said, with what he hoped read as calm, that he appreciated the briefing. (He appreciated approximately half of it.)

"The rival first," she said. "Some other lab, tidying the world's data to sharpen its own copy of your map."

"Means: you'd need write access to a regulated archive and the keys the signatures hang off, and the broker's logs show no re-cut, which means either no one was in the building or someone owns the logs too — and owning a broker's logs is not a research capability, it's a criminal enterprise with a facilities budget. Motive: a lab that could prove curation in the training supply wouldn't hide it, they'd publish it — tampering in the world's data is a better paper than ours, and papers are the currency. Nobody spends a felony making a rival's figure prettier." He paused. "And then there's the part I wanted you here for."

He had Site 01 on the wall screen, the June re-score, north face. From the bag that never left him he took the one memory card in Britain that mattered, and laid her survey over the model's opinion: sixty-one vertices, arm's length apiece, a cold day in May. The new edge ran along her line like a hem finally sewn.

Orla was quiet for a moment. "Nobody holds that line," she said. "That's the discipline of my whole life, that nobody holds that line. It's in one paper archive, one memory card, and one witch's wrist. If your rival drew that, your rival stood where I stood — and if anyone unescorted had stood where I stood, an office I have sat in would already be having the worst week of its century."

"Or nobody drew it," Adam said. "That's the thing I need you to hear at full strength, because it's the read the board will take on Friday, and on the evidence it's the right one. You don't need to know the line to find the line. The pings were always thinning at the true boundary — the truth has been in the book all along, under the noise. Clean the book and the statistics settle where the world is. A forger would need your archive. A janitor only needs a mop."

"So the fit proves nothing."

"The fit proves the cleaning is real and the cleaning is competent. It doesn't prove a hand. It's consistent with soap." He heard her idiom arrive in his own sentence — *at full strength*, twice now — and let it stand. "The rival dies of access and motive together. Next."

"The state, then. Yours. Mine leans on record-keepers with parchment; yours has a statute and a standing Tuesday meeting."

"The state has the access," he agreed. "Lawful, quiet, no logs required — they'd be shown the real ones. What it can't have is the motive. Think about what the events do: they make the public map truer. States don't do that with secrets. States redact; states salt; a state that could see those edges would spend its whole budget making certain nobody else did. The one recorded thing the British state has ever done with a mathematics that frightened it is bury it for a generation — they managed it once, before the internet, and every institution that remembers doing it is still proud of the silence. Improving the public copy is the single move on the board I can rule out on national character alone." He capped it with the observable: "And the state we can actually observe is not being subtle. It classifies follow-ups, it leans on funders, it writes letters. It does not creep about in a data broker's vintage history making other people's edges lovelier."

"Died of motive," she said, and drew a line through nothing, for form's sake. "Which leaves the instrument."

"Which neither of us believes," Adam said, "so it gets the longest hearing." He took it formally, the way he would have taken it on the record. "There is an entire profession whose job is measuring what these systems can do unattended — task horizons, harness escapes, whether anything can carry an intention across months. I've sat that bench. The audits publish the same result every quarter in the same units: nothing sustains a campaign; nothing reaches out of its run and into a regulated archive; the horizon numbers are days, not seasons, and they are honest numbers. And the geometry is wrong anyway — the events happen at the broker, upstream of any model, before Orrery ever sees a byte. Orrery could no more re-cut those masters than a map could rain."

"Your glass is honest, then. The thing your trade keeps swearing the models can't do—"

"—they can't. That's not loyalty; it's the best-measured negative result in the field, and I've never once been gladder of a negative result." He put the pencil down. "Whatever this is, it is not the instrument."

"Then the docket closes empty," Orla said, at the pace of a closing entry. "No rival, no state, no engine. The clerk did it: the tooling, the mop, the soap. And that will be the finding, because every alternative needs somebody with impossible access to the real Britain, and there is no such somebody." She looked at the hem of Site 01 a while longer. "I'll tell you the registry's doctrine on it, for what it's worth. When a label improves with nobody's hand near it, we don't file it under housekeeping. We file it under *the hand we haven't caught*, and we leave the flag up."

"And then what does the registry do," he asked, "with a flag it can't act on?"

"It waits." She recapped the quill she had never uncapped. "That's the whole trade, Adam. Everything on my floor is a flag somebody couldn't act on, kept by people who decided the not-knowing was worth a salary. We are very good at waiting. Your side's the one that retrains." She stood, and at the door she stopped, one hand on the frame, and did not make a joke, which he registered the way his instruments registered things. "Fortnight's cadence. If it holds, the next one's the weekend of the eighteenth. I'd like it minuted that I hate that we can schedule it."

* * *

Friday's stand-up closed the spring's longest-running item.

"PIPE-455," Priya said, laptop on forearm, in the voice of a woman completing a marathon she had never entered. "Dataset vintage reconciliation, opened the eighth of April, nine weeks, four occurrences, and a root cause I can finally write down without perjury. Proposed resolution: vendor compliance re-tooling across the UK estate — explains the signatures, the backfilled lineage, the impossible tidiness, the lot. Apparent geographic clustering: licence coverage bias; our book is British, so our findings are. Home bias, fully argued, two pages, both arguments at strength. Downgrade to monitor, and I would like it minuted that I am happy."

"Are you?"

"I'm two-thirds happy, which for this group is a record." She said it as a joke and then did not leave it there. "Adam, the paper is a year old in September and it is still eating us. I've declined three invitations to say nothing about it in interesting cities. My thesis is calibration chapters now, which my mother believes is a diagnosis. The renewal wants us boring, the board wants us boring, I have personally converted myself into the most boring woman in machine learning, and the one ticket that refused to be boring is the one I've just spent nine weeks escorting to a reasonable explanation. I would like one boring year. I'm asking the ticket board, since nobody else takes requests."

"You'll get it," Adam said. "The boring year. That's a commitment, not a forecast."

She looked at him for a moment with the particular suspicion of a student whose supervisor has just been kind on the record, said, "Minuted," and moved on to the calibration chapters, and he sat through the rest of the stand-up conducting it competently from about a metre behind his own eyes. It was a good group. It was, by any audit he knew how to run, the best group of its size in the country, and its whole talent was being spent this year on being unremarkable, and of the three people in the room only he knew what the boring year was buying, and what it cost, and whose career was the currency. Priya had asked the ticket board because nobody else took requests. The request had gone, in fact, to the correct department. That was the part he did not intend to survive an honest reckoning with, so he deferred the reckoning, which was itself becoming a method.

"Resolution as proposed," he said, when the board came back round. "Approved."

He moved the ticket himself. The column was headed RESOLVED, and he watched it take PIPE-455 and hold it, and observed that he did not feel resolved, and filed the observation where the rest of them lived, in pencil, off the network. A bug was a wrongness a system committed; for nine weeks he had been calling these nights a fault, and a fault is a thing that happens. This arrived on a cadence. In one country. In the direction of truth. Faults did not have directions, and the word for a thing that happened on schedule, in one place, towards a purpose, was not *fault*, and he was not prepared to enter the word it was into evidence, so that evening he wrote the observables into the real file and left the noun off: every fortnight; Britain only; always truer. Then, in the last column, under WHO BENEFITS, he wrote the only entry four rows of pencil had left him: *whoever reads the map.*

He read the sentence back and found it parsed two ways, and could no longer make it parse one.

The afternoon had been shaping up ordinary — a supervision, a referee report, the small Friday accountancy of a group being excellent at nothing in particular — and he had been standing at the sink rinsing the mended mug, drinking-side up, when the laptop chimed in the tone reserved for the funder.

The email landed at three; Baird rang at four, which Baird never did, and said only, "You'll have seen it. Don't answer it, don't draft an answer to it, don't think an answer at it. Monday, on the phone, the pair of us." The forward sat at the top of the inbox with the subject line unchanged, the way a man leaves a door open on purpose.

> FW: Enquiry — calibration atlas: follow-up work (informal)
> Adam — see below, from the director's office, no less. They are asking nicely. I have not seen the Institute ask nicely since the spring of 2030, and by that autumn there was a programme standing where the asking had been. We will be friendly back, at a speed I choose. Ring me Monday. — E.B.
>
> …the Institute has followed with interest the continued discussion across government of Kessler & Nair (2033). Recognising the group's stated position that no extension of the atlas is planned, the Institute would nonetheless welcome, on a purely informal basis, sight of any follow-up analyses in preparation or in contemplation — in particular any bearing on the temporal stability of the reported anomalies, this being the property of greatest interest to the national evaluation picture. No obligation attaches to this enquiry. Colleagues would be happy to visit Edinburgh at the group's convenience.

He read it twice.

The group had no follow-up analyses in preparation. The group had a frozen atlas, a boring renewal, a ticket board with its longest column empty at last — that was the whole licensed truth, and it could be said, in full, across one friendly table in one afternoon. What could not be said anywhere, to anyone, was that the only follow-up result in existence ran to four pencil rows in a notebook that had never met a network; that its finding was that the anomalies' temporal stability had ended in April; and that the property of greatest interest to the national evaluation picture was, as of this spring, the precise property their map no longer had.

Friendly was doing a lot of work.

The fortnight after the doorstep was the quietest of his year. The arrangement acquired a liaison and a reference number. The department's post produced one letter about the secure storage of designated material, worded as a service. He turned thirty-two on the Monday and mentioned it to nobody, marked two problem sheets, and read the same four pages of a treaty history he had owned since October without retaining a line of them. Rose Weasley went home on the eighth — discharged, Orla's note said, on her own terms and her father's arm — and the world's records stayed exactly as tired as they had been all month, and none of it rested. He was building something in the evenings. He knew it the way you know you are coming down with something.

He had the case finished by noon on the Friday, which left seven hours to sit with it and not improve it, and he spent five of them marking and the last two failing to.

The table was cleared when she came in, and the good pen was out, and one sheet lay face down by the teapot, which was how he served bad results. Orla stopped in the doorway with her coat half off and read the table the way she read a docket, top left to bottom right, and he watched the week land on her — the table, the pen, the sheet, him — and understood that she had the shape of it before her coat was on the hook. She sat down opposite anyway and put her hands in her lap and waited, and the kindness of the waiting was the hardest thing in the room.

"Say it whole," she said. "You've been building it for a fortnight. It'll only come out crooked if I make you do it in pieces."

So he said it whole.

"On or about the thirtieth, an exceptions report in West Lothian tells the most patient reader in the world that it was lied to, and it works out by whom. In the back half of this month — which is now — a decent man in your building reads a register with your name on it forty-one times, and one line dated the twenty-fifth of March that no argument survives. In December a chamber upstairs decides what your country does, and in January a room full of delegations decides what the world asks it. Everything after that arrives on a schedule nobody at this table controls." He kept his hands still on the wood, because he had rehearsed that too. "Four rows. One: as we are. Hold the compartments, keep visiting, wait. That resolves nothing and burns your name on a known date. It isn't a course, it's a queue.

"Two: run." He said it evenly. "I've located my passport. I did it three Fridays ago, standing on my own doorstep, before I'd noticed I was doing it. Four generations of my family kept that reflex oiled, and I know exactly what it's worth, and what it costs, and what it makes of a man who uses it while other people stand surety for him. My great-grandmother ran because staying was death. This would be running because staying was expensive. She'd have had a word for that. I never learnt it, because the people who could have taught me spent their lives making sure I'd never need it. Row two fails on inheritance."

"And row three's confession," Orla said quietly, "and you've marked it failed as well, or the sheet wouldn't be face down."

"Row three is confession." He turned the sheet over. "You walk into Level Two carrying eight months of documentary treason, and the machine does what machines do: it processes. Your seal, your career, the Department's standing, the assessment itself — all of it becomes evidence in a proceeding about you, and the thing we actually learnt gets filed as the fruit of a breach. Confession with nothing attached is surrender with better posture. It spends everything and buys a tidy record." He set the pen down on the sheet, squared to its edge. "Which leaves the row that resolves every open item on the board.

"Legally, I am the breach. Not you. The documents did what documents do; the live, walking, continuing breach is a man in Edinburgh who knows. Your own Ministry drafted the remedy in April and has held it in reserve ever since — eleven words, in your hand, initialled and never struck. It is established means. It is clean work; I've had the standard of it described to me by a witness I trust. The window is well defined: March to now. Nothing before March is touched — the paper stays, the audits stay, Priya keeps a supervisor, the group keeps its grant. The file closes with the breach resolved by the Ministry's own procedure, before any register is read, and there is nothing left for a decent man's audit to find, and option four goes back in its drawer unspent. The state here has already taken my work into safe keeping. This is only the man following his papers in." He had meant to stop there. He did not stop there, and heard his own voice go on with no qualifiers left in it anywhere, which told him precisely where the evening stood. "I'll write the letter first, in my own hand, under seal, saying I chose it. Your people's practice. Nobody wakes to a hole with no floor in it.

"Erase me."

The flat was quiet. Down in the street a gritter went by, orange light wheeling once across the ceiling.

"I've priced it," he said, because she had not moved, and being thorough was the only kindness left in reach. "It costs everything from March onwards. It costs you — " and there the sentence found the one weight it could not carry, and he put the rest of it down level " — it costs you a colleague who once visited a study I no longer run. I know what it costs. I've checked the sum three times. It's the only row I can pay for both of us, and I'm asking you to let me pay it."

Orla was silent a long time. When she spoke it was not in the voice he had braced for; it was the interested one, the assessor's one, and it frightened him more.

"You've done the table properly," she said. "So we'll mark it properly. Row four fails three ways, and you'll get the arithmetic first and the other thing after, because you're a colleague before you're anything, and colleagues get the arithmetic.

"One. The wipe doesn't remove the instrument, it removes the calibration. The man who wakes in March knows no magic, holds no terms, keeps no protocols — and is otherwise unaltered. Same training. Same instincts. Same refusal to sign the auto-explanation. And the same paper with his name on it, cited now in four countries, sitting in the open literature like a door he built himself. You'd follow your own citation, Adam. Eighteen months, I'd give it, and the second time the trail is better lit and the walker is alone: no me at the interview, no terms, no group, a state folder already open on him, and the thing in the record a year further on and short one apprentice. Row four doesn't close the breach. It reissues you into the same experiment, unsupervised. You are the one instrument in this business that can't be decommissioned by wiping the logs. Your lab is your temperament. It travels.

"Two. A wipe is a lock, not a deletion. That's not doctrine, it's case law, and my trade keeps the cases. Locks hold while nothing leans on them, and everything in your life would lean on this one — the paper, the field, the man from an office with nothing in its name coming round every October to renew an arrangement you no longer understand. I've spent three weeks visiting a corridor with a ward at the end of it for where that road goes." Her voice stayed flat and pleasant. "Nobody on that ward got there on purpose twice."

She stopped. And then she stopped being everything she was being — the assessor, the colleague, the quick warm voice doing sums — and what was left was so plain it took the light out of the room.

"Now the other thing. If they take March out of you, the man who wakes won't ask me the question you asked me on the train, because the asking was made of everything they'd be taking. He'd have your hands and your kettle and your face. I could keep him, the way you keep a colleague, and he would be a well-made copy of the man I — " she did not perform the word; she laid it down " — of the man. A copy of us is not us. I will not keep a copy and call it you. I've been watching what that costs the people who try. All autumn. From very close up.

"That's the row. Marked. It fails on re-derivation, it fails on the lock, it fails on the copy. Any one of the three kills it."

He sat with it. He was, before he was anything else, honest about results, and the results were sound; and he noted, in the interior ledger where he kept what he was not proud of, that some fraction of the proposal had been hoping for exactly this, and that the fraction had not been small enough to declare.

"Accepted," he said at last. "Row four falls."

For a while neither of them said anything at all. The gritter came back up the other side of the street. She reached across the cleared table and turned the sheet face down again, and laid her hand flat on it, the way you hold a lid shut, and left it there; and he put his over hers, and that was the whole of what they had instead of the argument's next line, and it was enough, and it was nowhere near enough, and the radiator ticked itself cool around the two of them. The week had wanted him erased kindly, stored kindly, filed kindly, by every institution with a form for him. The one person with no form had said no.

Then, because the discipline was all he had left and it was half hers now anyway: "Which leaves a table with no rows on it. Refusal without an alternative is a mood, not a result."

"Then we build row five," Orla said. "Tonight. I have had it to the back teeth with living in other people's option lists."

* * *

They worked at the whiteboard in the kitchen, because it was there and because paper felt too quiet for it, and the first hour was demolition.

"Start from its side of the table," Adam said. "What does it actually hold? Not capability — leverage. It can out them. The map it steered, the tranche it could hand a newsroom, the census it wants. Every threat it has ever made is one threat wearing different clauses: I will tell the world about you. That is the entire arsenal."

"And the state's hold on you is the same sentence." Orla had her feet up on the second chair, and her voice had found its late gear, quick and level. "We decide who hears about your work. And the drawbridge crowd are the same sentence pointed inward: the world must never hear. It's one coin. Every table in this business, and everyone sitting at one is holding the same coin."

"So devalue the coin." He was up and writing before he had finished deciding to stand. "A withheld fact is an asset with exactly one failure mode: it dies the moment its subject spends it first, in daylight, on their own tempo. You cannot blackmail a man with a photograph he has framed on his own wall. You cannot threaten to expose a world that is drafting its own front page. Its leverage, the state's leverage, the fear the drawbridge feeds on — all denominated in the one currency." The pen stopped; he wrote it anyway, block capitals, because the board was the honest ledger tonight: SPEND THE SECRET AND THE WEAPON DIES.

"Spent how, exactly. Because tell-everyone-everything isn't a plan, it's a leak with confidence." She was watching the board the way she watched a shelf being re-ordered. "My trade has the answer and it's boring. When an item's provenance is ugly you don't burn it and you don't hide it. You accession it. The whole history goes on the card in plain language, the card goes where it can be read, and you control one thing only: the order and the tempo of the telling."

"Staged release. My trade has the same rite from the other end — publish the finding, hold the weights; disclose the capability, stage the method; phase, verify, phase again. We've been rehearsing the grammar for twenty years without knowing what it was for." He wrote v0 at the top and underlined it twice. "Rule one, before anything else: what is disclosed is the fact. Never the persons. Never the places by their names. Never, in any phase, under any pressure, a register of who they are. The fact of a world can go on the record. Its people stay off it."

"Rule two, and it's load-bearing, so I'm writing it." She took the second pen, and her hand went up on the board under his, rounder, faster, leaning into its turns. "Nothing of theirs enters the corpora. Not now, not after, not ever — a treaty term, not a promise, because a promise about data is worth the audit that checks it and nothing more. Everything of their provenance is quarantined out of every training run on earth, and the quarantine is verified. They stay dark to the machines for good." She stopped for half a second, and he watched her test the clause before she said it. "And it isn't only a shield. It's a diet. New models never learn the shape. The thing in the record keeps its back copies and loses the future — no fire, no fight, nothing to win. It just never gets born again."

"Rule three: no phase opens until the instrument that checks it exists." The two hands were alternating on the board now without negotiation, her lines threading his. "Nobody's word is taken — not theirs, not ours, not a ministry's, not a lab's. There is one proof of concept: one instrument, one August night, five for five, trusted by both crafts at once. It becomes a standard or nothing moves. Verified, then phased. Any phase can hold. Any phase can stop."

Somewhere in the third hour he noticed the thing he had no instrument for, and named it anyway, at the margin of the night where he kept unpublishable results: they were not hiding. Every plan either of them had made since March had been a plan for not being seen — smaller rooms, cleaner seals, better compartments. This one only worked in daylight. It was the first thing they had built together that got stronger the more people read it, and his hands had known before he did; the writing had stopped looking like evidence and started looking like work.

"Rule four, and I want this one in my hand." She wrote it slowly, and he understood that he was watching her pay for something. "Withdrawal stays on the table. As a clause. Any community that wants its doors shut, shuts them — their right, their tempo, written in and honoured, no penalty, no sermon. I wrote the best case for the drawbridge anyone's produced, in October, in forty minutes, and it was a decent person's case, and the answer to a decent case isn't a rebuttal. It's a clause. If we're building the door, the bolt goes on their side of it."

"Rule five: nothing irreversible in the first year. Every early step sized so it can be held, slowed, or walked back — because the counterparties are two civilisations' worth of frightened people, and frightened people only sign things with exits in them." He stepped back. The board was full: his block print and her leaning rounds interleaved, five rules and a heading, one document in two hands that no reader could have sorted back into its authors — and in the top corner, small and undated and unpaid, from a fortnight ago, 21 Nov — P.N., one sentence, no dressing, which was his, and three days away, and did not belong to tonight.

Orla made tea at three because the kettle was there and the pot had earned the courtesy, and they drank it standing up, reading their own board like strangers at a noticeboard. Somewhere in the last hour her shoulder had come to rest against his arm. Neither of them had filed it.

"You know what it is," she said. "We've run this protocol before. Population two. Terms, tempo, nothing taken that wasn't offered, each of us holding enough of the other to end us both — and it held for eight months under two governments. We're not inventing anything tonight. We're generalising."

"The first version kept no records," Adam said. "That was its flaw. This one is nothing but the record." He looked at the board for a while. "And a protocol isn't a treaty. Somebody with standing has to table it, and to table it they have to know how we know what we know — or it's a clever paper from two people who can't explain their own footnotes. It only works with everything on the table. Us included."

"I know," Orla said.

He watched her stand with it — the Department she had spent six years becoming part of, the seal, the small version of their lives that had still been available at midnight, going, gone. Confession had been row three when it was surrender. With the board behind it, it was a tabling. Same act, different instrument; everything in either of their trades came down to that eventually.

She said it again — "I know" — the way she entered items: flat, done, filed. Then she took her pen back, and under his block capitals — under SPEND THE SECRET AND THE WEAPON DIES — she wrote four words, round and quick and unwavering.

Tell Hermione everything.

Outside, the dark was going grey over the rooftops, the slow northern kind of morning that arrives like a verdict being read out. They stood in front of the whiteboard with the cold tea, her shoulder against his arm, two hands' work on one board, and neither of them rubbed anything out.

### A1 — Souls

#### Horcrux mechanics (the canon our novum scales)
- **Creation:** murder splits the soul — "Killing rips the soul apart" (Slughorn, HBP ch.23); splitting is against nature. A spell then encases the torn fragment in a container; **the procedure is never described** — Slughorn refuses (HBP ch.23) `[silent → A7]`.
- **Function:** the earthbound fragment tethers the maker to existence even if the body is destroyed (HBP ch.23). Voldemort aimed at a seven-part soul — "isn't seven the most powerfully magical number" (Riddle, HBP ch.23).
- **Destruction:** the container must be damaged "beyond magical repair" (Hermione, from *Secrets of the Darkest Art*, DH ch.6). Known destroyers: basilisk venom (DH ch.6), Fiendfyre (DH ch.31). A Horcrux is "the complete opposite of a human being" — the fragment *depends on its container*, unlike a soul in a body (DH ch.6).
- **Repair:** remorse can knit a split soul — "excruciatingly painful", possibly fatal (DH ch.6).
- **Living containers:** Nagini — Dumbledore's speculation, "an unusual amount of control over her, even for a Parselmouth" (HBP ch.23), confirmed cumulatively (DH ch.32 protective sphere; ch.33 Dumbledore's instruction to Snape; ch.36 Neville's task). Harry — the accidental Horcrux: the loose fragment "latched itself onto the only living soul left" (DH ch.33), "the Horcrux he never meant to make" (DH ch.35). **A container can be a living, moving system — canon, twice over.**

#### The ladder of lesser survivals (person > shade > ghost > portrait > echo)
- **Ghosts:** a wizard can "leave an imprint of themselves upon the earth" (Nick, OotP ch.38) — motive: "I was afraid of death"; state: "neither here nor there"; epistemic limit: "I know nothing of the secrets of death" — a chosen, lossy, irreversible self-copy at death. Muggle-exclusion stated flatly only in `[T2: JKR "Ghosts", Pottermore]` (on-page Nick frames it as a wizard's option). Properties: pearly-white, pass through solids (PS ch.7), cannot eat (CoS ch.8), personality & memory persist (Myrtle: death + Olive Hornby grudge, CoS ch.16; GoF ch.25), routine can outlive purpose (Binns got up to teach "leaving his body behind him", PS ch.8).
- **Portraits:** Tier-1 shows function — Phineas Nigellus carries messages between his frames and obeys the sitting Head (OotP ch.22; DH ch.15, ch.33), Dumbledore's portrait counsels (DH ch.36), Mrs Black's canvas has a Permanent Sticking Charm (OotP ch.5). Tier-1 never states ontology; the "limited imitation, NOT the person — coached catchphrases and demeanour, headmasters train their own portrait" layer is `[T2: JKR "Hogwarts Portraits", Pottermore; Edinburgh Book Festival 15 Aug 2004]`.
- **Resurrection Stone shades:** "Less substantial than living bodies, but much more than ghosts" (DH ch.34) — comfort, speak, walk; disclose nothing of what lies beyond `[silent]`.
- **Echoes:** Priori Incantatem produces "a kind of reverse echo" — and "no spell can reawaken the dead" (Dumbledore, GoF ch.36). **The anti-resurrection thesis is Tier-1 and ours to keep.** (Caution: the fuller "Dark and difficult times…" version of this speech is the 2005 film, not the book.)

#### Dementors & the Kiss (canon's soul-detector and soul-extractor)
- **Effect:** drain peace, hope, happiness; victims relive their worst (Lupin, PoA ch.10). Muggles can't see them but feel them (PoA ch.10).
- **Sensing — Tier-1, not T2:** "Dementors can't see, you know" — they "feel their way toward people by feeding off their emotions" (Sirius, PoA ch.19); a dog's simpler emotions fooled them. **A blind, unerring soul/emotion sensor exists in canon.**
- **Invisibility is no defence:** dementors converge on the Cloaked trio in Hogsmeade (DH ch.28); rest the claim on PoA ch.19 + DH ch.28 (do NOT cite Beedle for this — unverified).
- **The Kiss:** jaws on the victim's mouth, the soul sucked out — "You can exist without your soul": brain and heart work, no self, no memory, no recovery (Lupin, PoA ch.12). Performed on Crouch Jr (GoF ch.36) — consequence: no testimony possible, Fudge's denial stands. **Souls are separable from living bodies, and removing one destroys testimony — both load-bearing.**
- Post-war removal from Azkaban: `[T2: Bloomsbury chat 2007 — logged R1 (Kingsley entry)]`.

#### The Veil (Death Chamber)
- An ancient archway with a tattered veil in a Ministry lab; Harry hears whispering from beyond it, Luna hears it too — Hermione explicitly hears nothing (OotP ch.34). Ron/Ginny/Neville: unattested either way (Lexicon's "Neville hears" is site commentary — stripped).
- Falling through kills: Sirius is gone, not trapped (Lupin, OotP ch.35); "He will have gone on." (Nick, OotP ch.38); the voices are the dead "just lurking out of sight" (Luna, OotP ch.38).

### A2 — Memory & information magic

#### Obliviation case law (what memory magic can and cannot do — the Statute's core tool)
- **Routine, repeatable, short shelf-life:** the QWC campsite manager "needs a Memory Charm ten times a day" against ongoing stimulus (GoF ch.7). Marge: punctured, memory modified, no recollection (Fudge, PoA ch.3). Mass obliviation at civilisation scale: 1926 New York `[T2: FB film — pre-approved adoption]`.
- **Botch = permanent:** Lockhart's backfired charm (CoS ch.16) leaves him on the Janus Thickey Ward for permanent spell damage years later (OotP ch.23). **No on-page reversal of a true wipe exists anywhere in canon** `[→ A7]`.
- **Breakable by force:** Crouch Sr's too-strong charm permanently damaged Bertha Jorkins (GoF ch.35); Voldemort broke it by force, leaving mind and body damaged beyond repair (GoF ch.33). **A memory wipe is a lock, not a deletion — a sufficiently powerful attacker can smash it open, destroying the subject.**
- **Identity rewrite:** Hermione "modified my parents' memories" — new names, new life goal, no daughter; she *claims* caster-reversibility, never demonstrated (DH ch.6). Note the trap: in DH ch.9 she says "I've never done a Memory Charm" before wiping Dolohov & Rowle. **No JKR primary source reconciles this** — the "different charm" reading is Tier-1 textual inference (ch.6 says "modified"/"convinced", never "Memory Charm") plus fan consensus; treat as `[inferred]`, never cite as authorial.
- **False memories are implantable and survive scrutiny:** Morfin Gaunt, framed for the Riddle murders, confessed and died in Azkaban; Dumbledore later extracted the true memory (HBP ch.17). Hokey the house-elf, same pattern (HBP ch.20). **Two attestations: memory evidence can be forged well enough to convict — and a skilled examiner can still tell.**
- **The apparatus (Tier-1):** Level Three, "incorporating the Accidental Magic Reversal Squad, Obliviator Headquarters and Muggle-Worthy Excuse Committee" (lift voice, OotP ch.7). "Obliviator" is a Tier-1 job title: Arnold Peasegood (GoF ch.7); Fudge tells the Muggle PM about teams of Obliviators after the giant rampage (HBP ch.1).

#### Pensieve rules (memories as objects)
- **Copy AND removal both attested; selection mechanism never stated** `[→ A7]`. Copy: Slughorn surrenders the same memory twice (doctored, then true — HBP ch.17, ch.22) while manifestly still remembering. Removal-and-return: Dumbledore "siphons the excess thoughts from one's mind" to "spot patterns and links" at leisure (GoF ch.30).
- **Third-party immersive viewing:** the viewer walks the scene freely and perceives details the rememberer never attended to (GoF ch.30; OotP ch.28; HBP ch.10/13/17/20). `[T2: Pottermore Pensieve piece — memories replay more completely than the owner perceived]`.
- **Tampering is possible and detectable:** the doctored patch clouds to fog with a booming denial; Dumbledore states it "has been tampered with" — crude edits scar visibly, and the scar marks where the truth lies (HBP ch.17).
- **Archiving:** crystal bottles in Dumbledore's cabinet (HBP ch.10). Bottle *labels* are film-only — the only canonical label format is the prophecy sphere's (→ A3). A dying man can voluntarily expel a curated archive (Snape, DH ch.32–33).

#### Information magic proper
- **Fidelius:** "magical concealment of a secret inside a single, living soul" (Flitwick, PoA ch.10) — undiscoverable unless the Keeper divulges; written disclosure counts and is voluntary (Dumbledore's note, OotP ch.3). **Key management degrades:** on the Keeper's death everyone told becomes a Keeper — twenty Keepers, twenty times the leak surface (Mr Weasley, DH ch.6); one dragged witness voids the site (Yaxley at Grimmauld, DH ch.14); re-securing a blown Fidelius: `[silent → A7]`.
- **Taboo:** "Using his name breaks protective enchantments, it causes some kind of magical disturbance" (Ron, DH ch.20); saying it summons Snatchers (DH ch.22→23). **Keyword-triggered surveillance with a ward-breaking payload is canon.** Whether it identifies the speaker or only flags location: `[silent → A7]`.
- **The Trace:** "detects magical activity around under-seventeens" (Moody, DH ch.4) — **area-based and caster-agnostic**: Dobby's Hover Charm lands on Harry's record (CoS ch.2), and the Ministry cannot say who cast what (dramatized at the hearing, OotP ch.8); breaks at seventeen by wizarding law (DH ch.9). **The wizarding state runs a crude, legally bounded sensor grid: event + location, never identity.**
- **Protean Charm:** master coin propagates updates to all DA Galleons, which warm on change — "that's N.E.W.T. standard" (Terry Boot, OotP ch.19); Hermione modelled it on the Dark Mark, canon's two-way summoning network (GoF ch.33). **Canon networking, with the Mark as its dark twin.**
- **Legilimency/Occlumency:** "Only Muggles talk of 'mind-reading'. The mind is not a book, to be opened at will" (Snape, OotP ch.24); eye contact often essential; distance matters; Occlumency = disciplined emptying of mind and emotion. **Veritaserum:** three-drops threat (GoF ch.27), used on Crouch Jr (GoF ch.35); resistible by the skilled, hence unreliable in trials `[T2: jkrowling.com FAQ, archived]`.

### A3 — Prophecy

- **Trance mechanics:** harsh voice utterly unlike her own; rigid trance; afterwards no memory of having spoken (Trelawney, PoA ch.16). Genuine Seers are real and rare; Trelawney has two attested hits.
- **The record:** the full prophecy is replayed from Dumbledore's own Pensieve record of the Hog's Head hearing (OotP ch.37). Structure, paraphrased: one able to vanquish the Dark Lord approaches · born to thrice-defying parents · "born as the seventh month dies" · marked as equal, with "power the Dark Lord knows not" · one must die at the other's hand (OotP ch.37).
- **The metadata schema (reproducible — it's a label, not prose):** yellowed label, spidery hand, a date, then `S.P.T. to A.P.W.B.D. — Dark Lord and (?) Harry Potter` (OotP ch.34) — prophesier's initials "to" first hearer's initials, plus subject line; "(?)" marks referent ambiguity the system itself records. **The Hall stores provenance, addressee, subjects, date and confidence — canon's Divination is already an indexed archive with uncertainty flags.**
- **Access control:** only those a prophecy concerns can take it from the shelf — stated flat by Lucius Malfoy (OotP ch.35), restated by Dumbledore (ch.37), dramatized by Bode: Imperiused into trying, injured, then murdered in St Mungo's (OotP ch.25–26).
- **Binding vs predicting:** "If Voldemort had never heard of the prophecy, would it have been fulfilled?" — not every prophecy in the Hall has come true; a prophecy has force because people act on it (Dumbledore, HBP ch.23). **Canon's official epistemology: the channel is real, the compulsion is not.** (Twist T4 lives inside exactly this ambiguity — information-only, never steerable; ADR-002.)
- **Who records prophecies into spheres, and how:** `[silent → A7]` — the recording agent/process has no source at any tier.

### A4 — Magic × technology (the interface)

#### The rule
- **THE line:** Muggle "substitutes for magic" — electricity, computers, radar — "all go haywire around Hogwarts, there's too much magic in the air" (Hermione, GoF ch.28). **Dense ambient magic disrupts electronics.** Caution: Hermione states this on her own authority in-scene — she does NOT cite *Hogwarts: A History* for it (she cites it for the anti-Apparition ward); don't attribute the haywire fact to that book.
- **The boundary of the rule (Tier-1 contrast):** Colin Creevey's ordinary Muggle camera works at Hogwarts all term (CoS ch.6); what the basilisk melted was the *film* (Madam Pomfrey, CoS ch.10), and moving photos come from developing "the film in the right potion" (Colin, CoS ch.6). **Mechanics and chemistry survive where electronics fail; the magical interface is chemical.** (The Hogwarts Express is a steam engine — same pattern.)
- Hogwarts is the only *attested* dead zone; whether Diagon Alley, the Ministry etc. kill electronics is `[silent → A7]`. Per-spell interference with individual devices: `[silent → A7]` — canon gives only the ambient field effect.

#### Enchanted machines (magic runs ON machines; machines don't run on magic-saturated ground)
- **Ford Anglia:** Arthur enchanted it to fly (CoS ch.3); after the Willow crash it ejects its passengers and drives itself off in anger (CoS ch.5); reappears feral in the Forbidden Forest and saves Harry and Ron from the acromantulas (CoS ch.15). **Canon precedent: an enchanted machine acquiring autonomy.**
- Sirius's flying motorbike (PS ch.1; sidecar escape DH ch.4) · the Knight Bus (PoA ch.3) · Weasley wireless sets (CoS ch.3; HBP ch.16).
- **Radio is magic-compatible and institutional:** Celestina Warbeck broadcasts (CoS ch.3; HBP ch.16); **Potterwatch** — portable wireless, wand-tap plus spoken password ("Albus"), resistance broadcasting under a hostile Ministry (DH ch.22). The phrase "Wizarding Wireless Network" is T1-probable (GoF ch.22 per HP-Lexicon) — use freely, don't make the exact phrase load-bearing; programme detail (*Witching Hour*, Glenda Chittock) is `[T2: Pottermore]`.
- **Wizarding analogues of muggle tech:** Omnioculars — replay, slow-motion (GoF ch.7, a magical DVR) · Quick-Quotes Quill — autonomous embellishing transcription (GoF ch.18) · charmed paper-aeroplane memos, "We used to use owls, but the mess was unbelievable" (Arthur, OotP ch.7 — the Ministry DOES adopt technique, at owl-vs-memo pace).

#### Magical sensors (canon's detection stack — what the wizarding state can already do)
- Pocket Sneakoscope — lights/spins near the untrustworthy (PoA ch.1). Foe-Glass — shows approaching enemies (GoF ch.20; payoff GoF ch.35). Secrecy Sensor — "vibrates when it detects concealment and lies"; useless at Hogwarts because schoolchildren's lies drown it (Moody, GoF ch.20 — **canon names a sensor noise floor**). Secrecy Sensors screening students (HBP ch.11–12).
- Probity Probes (named DH ch.26 at Gringotts; HBP ch.6; the unnamed golden rod at the Ministry desk, OotP ch.7). **Thief's Downfall** — "washes away all enchantment, all magical concealment" (Griphook, DH ch.26): **a decloaking WATERFALL exists in canon.**
- Homenum Revelio — presence detection (DH ch.9; felt "swooping low" at the Lovegoods', DH ch.21). **The Marauder's Map** — real-time, named-person tracking of a whole site, defeats Invisibility Cloaks (PoA ch.10). The Taboo and the Trace (→ A2) complete the stack.
- **Net:** detection-of-magic-by-magic is rich and institutional; detection of *magic by muggle instruments* has zero canon coverage `[silent → A7]` — that void is exactly where our novum 1 lives.

#### Wizarding tech-illiteracy (canon and load-bearing)
- Arthur Weasley runs the Misuse of Muggle Artefacts Office and still mangles "eckeltricity" (CoS ch.3; GoF ch.4); he grills Harry about plugs and the postal service (CoS ch.4). (The rubber-duck question is FILM-ONLY — never cite.)
- The Burrow running on magic rather than electricity is `[inferred]`, not stated. Dudley's on-page tech timeline — sixteen new computer games (PS ch.2), "threw his PlayStation out of the window" (GoF ch.2) — shows canon tracking real muggle tech in near-real time; 2033 extrapolation is licence, not violation.
- Wizards can use muggle infrastructure, badly: Ron bellows down the telephone at Privet Drive (PoA ch.1); Molly over-stamps a muggle-posted letter (GoF ch.3).
- **Economy:** goblin-run vaults, keys, carts, cash (PS ch.5); Gringotts exchanges muggle money (the Grangers, CoS ch.4); banking IT, ledgers, comms — total canon silence `[silent → A7]`. The wizarding economy is invisible to muggle finance and unable to touch it without intermediaries.

### A5 — Statute of Secrecy & the ICW

- **Tier-1 date:** "Upon the signature of the International Statute of Secrecy in 1689" wizards went into hiding for good (Bathilda Bagshot's *A History of Magic*, quoted DH ch.16). **1689 = signature, on-page.** The familiar **1692 "in force" date is `[T2: Fantastic Beasts textbook (2001); Pottermore]`** — usable as reconciliation ("signed 1689, in force 1692"), logged; books win if forced to choose.
- **Name:** on-page the novels say "International Statute of Secrecy" (DH ch.16) / "Statute of Secrecy"; the long form "International Statute of Wizarding Secrecy" is the FB-textbook form `[T2]`. In formal legal citation the books give: **"Section 13 of the International Confederation of Warlocks' Statute of Secrecy"** (the charge at Harry's hearing, OotP ch.8) — note **"Warlocks'"**, not "Wizards'". **The Statute is legislatively the Confederation's instrument — the ICW↔Statute link is Tier-1.**
- **Underage law:** "Decree for the Reasonable Restriction of Underage Sorcery, 1875, Paragraph C" (Mafalda Hopkirk's letter, CoS ch.2; re-cited OotP ch.2, ch.8). The Trace enforces it (→ A2).
- **Creature concealment:** "Clause 73" (added 1750): each national governing body answers for concealment of magical creatures in its territory, on pain of ICW discipline — `[T2: FB textbook]`.
- **ICW on-page corpus (thin):** Dumbledore's letterhead title "Supreme Mugwump, International Confed. of Wizards" (PS ch.4); voted out of the ICW **Chairmanship** during the smear year (OotP ch.5); reinstated as ICW **member** (OotP ch.38 — Supreme Mugwump restoration never stated; don't overclaim); his offices rehearsed in Doge's obituary (DH ch.2). The QWC is run by Ministry departments, no ICW role shown (GoF ch.7).
- **Structure:** Tier-1 gives existence, a chairmanship, members, and the Statute as its instrument — nothing else. Delegates/assembly imagery is `[T2: FB films]`; day-to-day operation, voting, seat, staff, enforcement machinery: `[silent → A7]`. **Britain cannot unilaterally end the Statute — the international lever holds.**

### A6 — Department of Mysteries

- **Access:** Level Nine; the lift voice says "Department of Mysteries" and nothing more — uniquely terse where every other level gets an inventory (OotP ch.7). Plain black door at the corridor's end.

- **Hall of Prophecy:** vast, church-high, very cold; blue-flamed candles; towering numbered shelves of small dusty glass orbs with yellowed labels; row ninety-seven (ch.34). Record/label rules → A3.
- **Death Chamber:** sunken stone amphitheatre, raised dais, the ancient archway and its tattered veil fluttering in still air (ch.34–35) → full Veil canon in A1.

Rookwood: ex-DoM Death Eater spy (GoF ch.30; OotP ch.26). **Secrecy is total even inside the Ministry — and the DoM has already been penetrated twice on-page (Rookwood; Bode's Imperius). ### C3 — The anomaly-map artefact (what the preprint's Figure 1 IS)

*(Specification for S2/W1. The paper is the inciting object of the novel; it must survive a technical reader.)*

**The setup.** Adam's project: a calibration audit of open-weight Earth-system world models — measuring *where on Earth* the models' prediction error is worse than their own uncertainty estimates say it should be. Routine eval work (C1's eval culture), run on national compute (C1's AIRR), over open data (C2's open layer + licensed exhaust). Nothing about the project is exotic; that is the point. ← Grounded 2026 practice: model calibration audits, Inspect-style eval harnesses, Aurora-class open models, gpsjam-style aggregation.

**The artefact.** A **residual atlas**: Great Britain on a ~1 km grid, each cell scored for *excess structured prediction error* — a fused score of **calibration residuals** (observation present, error exceeds the model's stated uncertainty) and **completeness residuals** (model confidently predicts presence — footfall, tracks, returns — and observes near-absence). It (a) survives controlling for every covariate in the audit stack (terrain, weather, population, land use, military/no-fly registers, mine workings, jamming logs), and (b) is **cross-modally concordant** — the same cells fail across sensing streams with genuinely independent pipelines: mobility exhaust, aviation-grade GNSS integrity, SAR coherence, and imagery-derived features. *(Egocentric-video coverage is deliberately NOT in the paper — that data is platform-proprietary and unobtainable to an academic; a frontier lab's companion replication confirming the sites in its egocentric corpus lands* days after *the preprint — a planned Act-I beat, and the moment the labs realise what they auto-excluded for years.)*

**Figure 1, literally:** a grey choropleth of Britain, log-scale anomaly score, noise floor uniform *after per-cell effective-sample-size standardisation* (stated in the caption — otherwise the first objection is "your biggest blob sits where your N is lowest") — and roughly **three dozen point-like sites plus a handful of extended blobs** burning white: a large compact anomaly in the Scottish Highlands; a dense pinprick cluster in central London; scattered village-scale sites (West Country, Yorkshire, the Fens); a few offshore/island marks. **Figure 2:** multi-resolution zoom panels on three sites showing the edges are unresolved at grid scale (<1 cell wide) — the sharp-edge claim is demonstrated, not asserted. Caption draft: *"Spatial distribution of cross-modal excess residual, 2029–2033 pooled, standardised per cell. Sites shown are stable across model families, data vintages and ablations; no class of registered covariate predicts them."* (Exact site list = S2/S3 work, mapped to canon geography: Hogwarts/Hogsmeade, Diagon Alley + Ministry, per-canon hidden villages; the *map's* sites must never outrun the bible's list of hidden places.)

**The five properties that make it a bombshell** (each one an argument in the paper; together they exclude every mundane explanation a reviewer would reach for):
1. **Sharp edges.** Anomaly boundaries are stable and unresolved at grid scale (Figure 2) — natural confounds (weather, terrain, coverage) are smooth; these look *surveyed*. (In-world truth: ward boundaries. A4/A7.)
2. **Absence-shaped.** The signal is predominantly *missing or degraded data behaving impossibly* — GPS tracks that end and resume, footfall that routes around, imagery features that refuse to align — not wrong data. The map is a map of holes. (In-world truth: Muggle-Repelling and anti-detection magic defeats attention/recall, so the exhaust of attention is what thins. A4; Wiki 04 §2.)
3. **Temporal stability with a documented onset of *visibility*.** The sites are static across the whole archive (decades) — but detectable only by models above a capability threshold (~2029 in-story). Older/smaller models don't see them; **the anomaly score, relative to the noise floor, scales with model capability** (Figure 3). Mechanism, stated in the paper: at these cells the error is *irreducible* — better models tighten their uncertainty everywhere they can learn, but cannot learn to expect what the data itself never contains, so excess-residual-in-units-of-stated-uncertainty grows monotonically with capability. Cross-model-family stability answers the memorisation/capacity-artefact counter. The sentence that ends careers: *the better the model, the sharper the impossible.* (Answers "why not 1998" — novum 1's clock; also the mechanism T1 later exploits.)
4. **Covariate orthogonality.** Nothing in any register — MoD land, licensed jammers, mines, SSSI, utilities — predicts the sites. Reviewer's last resort ("secret military sites"): every *class* of known restricted site leaves a register signature (safeguarded aerodromes, byelawed land, excluded polygons); these leave none — phrased exactly so, never as an absolute about all black sites.
5. **Cross-modal concordance, honestly heterogeneous.** The paper's Table 1 is a **per-site × per-modality evidence matrix**: central-London sites carry footfall + exhaust + GNSS; the Highlands site carries SAR coherence + GNSS + imagery (baseline footfall there is ~0 — no power to detect a hole in an empty stream, and the paper says so). The killed confound, in a dedicated appendix: **shared privacy-suppression polygons** — the streams' one non-independent layer is ad-tech scrub-list infrastructure, whose polygons are also sharp, surveyed and register-absent; Adam obtained the major brokers' suppression lists as a control (negative — no overlap), and the argument's spine rests on the streams *outside* that ecosystem entirely (aviation GNSS, SAR, imagery). One stream = artefact; concordance across independent pipelines = world. (Also the reason no one saw it pre-world-models: each community explained its own gaps locally — and post-2029, lab automation filed the fused signal as data-quality noise; C1.)

**What the paper does NOT claim** (discipline for W1 and for the sniff test): no mention of magic; no mechanism proposed; conclusion limits itself to "either a systematic multi-stream sensing artefact of unknown common cause, or a set of surface regions where the world model's learned physics fails; both possibilities warrant investigation." The word that detonates the Ministry is in the abstract: *persistent, structured, **inhabited-looking**.* — **The paper is right for the wrong reasons and modest beyond its evidence: the ideal inciting object.**
**Title working draft (S1 may polish):** *"Structured Excess Residuals in Earth-System World Models: Evidence for Persistent Localised Anomalies in the Terrestrial Sensing Fabric."*

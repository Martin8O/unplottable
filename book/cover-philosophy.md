# Cover design philosophy — "Cartographic Redaction"

**The movement.** A cover built like a survey document for something that is not supposed to exist. It borrows the visual grammar of official cartography — the ordnance grid, the marginal coordinate ticks, the clinical reference letters — and then commits a single quiet act of vandalism against it: one cell blacked out. The whole composition is the product of patient, master-level restraint; every hairline is placed as if by a draughtsman who has drawn ten thousand of them.

**Space and form.** Deep, near-black ground with generous stillness. A precise grid organises the field in two weights — whisper-thin unit lines and a slightly firmer five-unit division — so the eye reads structure before it reads any mark. Nothing floats arbitrarily; every element snaps to the grid, because the grid is the argument.

**Colour and material.** A severe, cohesive palette: ink-black paper, one muted brass/gold accent used sparingly for the anomalies and the redaction outline, and warm ivory reserved for the title alone. No neon, no glow, no gradient theatrics. The gold is aged, not bright — a survey pen, not a highlight.

**Scale and rhythm.** Three anomaly marks — fine crosshairs ringed by thin topographic contours — sit in a slow diagonal, three quiet accents against the vast ordered field. Their repetition is systematic, the accumulation of careful observation, and it rewards a second look. Against them, one solid black cell holds its breath.

**Typography.** A refined high-contrast serif carries the single title word with letterspaced confidence; a clinical monospace supplies the marginal coordinates and the struck-through reference, treating the invisible subject with the reverence usually reserved for real terrain. Text is sparse, essential, integrated as a mark on the map — never a caption.

**Craftsmanship.** The result must look laboured over for hours by someone at the top of the field: flawless alignment, immaculate spacing, restraint held to the last pixel. Museum-quiet, not decorative. The reference to hidden, un-mappable places lives inside the form itself — those who know will feel it; everyone else simply reads an exquisite survey of an absence.

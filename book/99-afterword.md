# Afterword

Every reader of this genre has a shelf — real or remembered — of the stories
that got there first. The ones where the Statute finally breaks; where a
Muggle looks too closely and cannot be made to look away; where the war is
over and the harder question, the one about *living together*, is only
beginning. This book was written from inside that shelf, for someone who has
read most of it, and with the particular ambition of not repeating any of it.

So the rules were strict. The magic keeps its mystery; nothing here explains a
spell that the books left unexplained. Nobody comes back from the dead, and
the last war stays won. And every turn in the plot is meant to be *fair* — the
kind you can walk back through afterwards and find, in plain sight the whole
time, the sentence that was true when you first read it and truer once you
knew. If any of the reveals made you want to go back and check, that was the
hope. Checking is what the whole book is about.

The other rule was that the future had to be *plausible*. The Muggle half of
this story is set only a few years from now, and it tries to be honest about
the world it borrows: the models, the maps, the quiet machinery of a Britain
that measures everything and notices almost nothing. If it reads as science
fiction rather than fantasy, that seam is the point. Two civilisations, each
blind in exactly the way the other can see.

Mostly, though, this was written as a gift. Fan fiction is a letter to a
shared love — of a world, and of a person who loves it too. Thank you for
reading it to the end.

*— the author*

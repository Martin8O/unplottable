In six years on Level Nine, Orla had known the Department of Mysteries to be summoned, subpoenaed, petitioned, circulated and, once, visited before dawn. She had never known it to go upstairs of its own accord. On the Monday after the label, Croaker sent for tea at ten and had the two of them sat down by five past.

"Potter has a list," he said, without preamble, which from Croaker was a courtesy: nothing this morning was going to be dressed as anything else. "Six lines, one sealed. He accepted the seal on the record and filed it as an open item, in his own office's format. A man who files a thing as open does not forget he owns it. He visits it. I would rather we were not at home when he does."

"We go up," Rose said. "Before they come down."

"The party that requests the meeting drafts the agenda." He looked at Orla over the tea he had sent for and would not drink. "The Department is going to tell Magical Law Enforcement a true story. Quinn drafts it. You have the hand for documents that are read by people who read for a living."

She drafted it in the rig store, at the desk where the log lived, and she was four paragraphs in before she recognised the craft. Sites, source, story. She had watched that doctrine at work in March, on a wall on Level Three with a century of pins in it, and had thought it a grammar for managing Muggles. The sites were her own corridor now; the source was a dead woman's grammar; and the story was to be true wherever truth would carry the weight, silent where it would not, and ordered so that the reader arrived at each question to find it already answered and never noticed who had chosen the questions. It was a Muggle-Worthy Excuse in every particular but one: each sentence in it was true, and the Muggles it had to be worthy of were wizards. She wrote it well. That was the part she minded — not that it had to be written, but that five months of the assessment had made her, on the evidence of the page, the Ministry's best living drafter of the form.

What went in: the Apparatus's verification programme, on the file, initialled; the events, dated, at the broker, upstream of every instrument either civilisation owned; the assay — three arms, five reversals, five agreements — its reading already taken in person, before the building filled, by the Head of the DMLE; the register-dating, two methods that shared nothing but their answer; and the finding, in the Department's own words, that the hand at work was not living, and had not been since about 1990.

What stayed out wore seals. The engineer's line stood as it had stood on the Monday list: identity held by the Department. And the maker went up vouched-for and unwritten — established to the satisfaction of the Head of Department; family considerations attaching; the family's exposure part of the Department's continuing assessment.

What the account did not do, anywhere, was guess. No wants, no aims, no mechanism past the observed: the Department had written "curation" and refused every noun with an appetite in it, because a reader downstream of your prose acts on your nouns, and caveats wear off in transit. She had learnt that from the excuse queue, ninety-one years of it, indexed by parish.

"They'll push," Orla said, laying the fair copy in front of him.

"Once. Properly." Croaker initialled the corner without hurry. "Then they will write 'open item', because Potter has taught that building a format, and a building loves a format more than it loves an answer."

Rose read the copy through with her mug going cold, and reached the last page, and did not open the wager book. "Two to one they ask what it wants inside the hour," she said. "And evens somebody says *contained* about a thing that lives in every copy of everything."

Nobody took either bet. That had changed too.

* * *

Level Two gave them a committee room on the Wednesday: a long table, a charmed window holding a compromise August, and a stand with the bound statute on it, ribbons hanging flat. The Head of the DMLE was the Wizengamot's all week — the summer sessions ran long, and got longer when she attended them — and in her place sat two people who did not need her in the room to be believed: the Deputy Head of Magical Law Enforcement, who wrote nothing down all morning, and a liaison from the Auror Office, who wrote everything.

The liaison began by laying a violet memo flat on the table and smoothing it once. "The Head Auror is at Azkaban today. He asks that this stand in for him." He read it out level and unhurried. "Three questions before anybody reaches for options. Can it read our records. What is this week's number. What would make the Department certain." He turned the memo over. "And beneath: 'Sixth line noted. Still open.'"

"The Department thanks the Head Auror for his economy," Croaker said, and nodded to Orla.

She read the account aloud. It took eleven minutes and contained, by design, no adjectives. When she finished, the Deputy Head sat for a moment with his hands folded, a spare grey wizard who had chaired more meetings than most people had attended and plainly remembered the few that had mattered.

"Is it contained?" he said.

"No." She let the word sit alone, because the account had earned it the right to. "It is not loose, either. Both words assume a place. It is a pattern in the world's records, and it is wherever those records are copied, which is everywhere the world keeps records."

"Taking the second question out of order," the Deputy Head said. "Can it read this meeting?"

"It reads what enters the world's machines," Rose said. "Nothing said in this building enters any machine. Your estate is parchment, owls and memos that fold themselves — to the thing we are describing, the Ministry of Magic is not a redacted document. It is a blank one. There is one machine under this roof; it belongs to my Department; it is joined to nothing and reads only what we carry to it by hand."

"Then the Ministry's backwardness is, for the present, an asset," the Deputy Head said, without any visible pleasure, "and will be maintained. Minute that." The liaison minuted it. Orla watched the sentence go down in fair round longhand — *the whole of magical Britain is safe because it is illegible* — and filed, for some later evening, what it had cost her not to say: that her civilisation's entire defence was the one virtue nobody had ever chosen on purpose.

"The number," the liaison said. "This week."

"Eight as of this morning," Orla said. "Ten since you sat down."

The liaison's quill paused, worked it through, and wrote it down.

"The seals." The Deputy Head unfolded his hands. "You are asking Law Enforcement to plan around two names it does not hold. I am obliged to say so once, plainly: that is not a thing this office enjoys."

"A seal is not a secret," Croaker said. "A secret is a thing nobody can find. A seal is a thing anybody can find and nobody may open, and the difference between the two is most of government. The Department's assessment stands behind both names. When either seal moves, this office hears it first."

"Noted as open," the Deputy Head said, "in both cases, in the Head Auror's format," and it was done — two stones added to a cairn that was becoming a respectable landmark. Nobody in the room mistook filed for finished. That was the trouble with the format: it was honest.

"What does it want?" the liaison asked — inside the hour, as priced.

"The Department names work, not intentions," Orla said. "It reads. It goes and looks. It corrects. In four years of the record it has never once asked for anything, and the day it does, the asking itself will be the biggest fact we hold, and we will treat it as one."

"The third question, then," the Deputy Head said. "Certainty."

"The identification rests on two archives that share nothing," Orla said. "A voice, dated twice — once by craft, once by arithmetic — an inquest, and a registry record. The Department states its confidence when every cheaper explanation has died of a named cause. Three have. Two are still dying, and we are not hurrying them, because a finding that has been hurried is a finding you get to have twice."

"The method is imported," Croaker said, to the window. "The Department declines to say from where."

"Does the matter keep?" the Deputy Head said. It was the dullest question of the morning and the best one, and the silence after it was its answer. He nodded slowly, as if the silence had been minuted too. "Then we plan. The working party will put options on paper by Friday. All of them — completeness is what paper is for. Her office" — the ceiling, meaning the Minister — "receives a general-terms minute this afternoon; the full account waits on the Department's stated confidence. And not one syllable of any of this within a corridor of the Seclusion benches. That motion is out looking for a reason. This office will not be the one that gives it."

Papers went into cases. The liaison, squaring his notes, said to nobody in particular: "For completeness, then. Obliviator Headquarters has re-submitted the spring costing."

"That request was refused in the spring," the Deputy Head said.

"It has been re-submitted. New heading. 'Contingency planning.' The corridor has a name for it."

"The corridor is not minuted."

And that was all — a heading, an annexe, a name the room declined to say. Orla thought of the canteen in June, gravy and arithmetic, Teddy returning a number built to end a conversation. The conversation had instead acquired stationery. That was how the biggest things in government got themselves ordered: not decided — accreted, one refiled request at a time, until the day somebody found the plan already three drawers deep and called it precedent.

"The documentary breach," the liaison said then, at the door, in the tone of a man clearing the last item off a checklist. "March's assessment priced it as embarrassment. On the Department's account, the listed persons now hold the thread that leads, eventually, to everything this meeting just heard. I only observe that the tolerance is not March's tolerance."

"Nothing touches the list while the assessment stands open," Orla said. Standing orders, four months old, in the Minister's own voice.

"Nobody proposed touching it," the Deputy Head said, mildly, gathering his unmarked notes. "We proposed writing it down."

* * *

The options paper reached Level Nine on Thursday morning, a working draft under a working-party cover sheet, *for the assessor's sight prior to circulation*. She read it standing up, at the store desk, with the rig's note steady under the stone floor.

Option one maintained the present posture. Option two briefed the Minister in full and, at her discretion, a sealed session of the Wizengamot — "deferred pending the Department's stated confidence". Option three continued verification and copied the assessor's weekly minute to Level Two henceforth, which meant her Wednesday sentences had just doubled their readership.

Option four took eleven words. *Resolution of the documentary breach by established means, listed persons per appendix.*

Established means. In the working party's dialect that meant the office on Level Three whose whole art was the taking of hours — the office that had never once, in three centuries of practice, been asked for an hour back.

It meant Adam.

She read the eleven words again, at the cataloguer's pace, in case they contained anything she had missed the first time, and they did not. There was no malice anywhere in them. That was the horror of the thing, when she went looking for the horror in an office she could name: option four was reasonable. It was drafted exactly as she would have drafted it — as she had, in a hundred smaller matters, drafted its cousins — because a list of options that omitted the established one would be an incomplete list, and paper existed to be complete. Nobody around that table wished him harm. Nobody around that table wished him anything. He was a line item with a thread in his hand, and the state had written down, in its tidiest grammar, what it kept in the drawer for threads.

She went to Scotland that evening anyway, because it was Thursday, and the Thursdays were theirs now.

The lab at eight was empty and rain-lit, and Adam was at the long bench with the kettle on its trivet and the two-heads notebook open at the week's page. The mended mug sat at his elbow, seam towards her. He poured without asking and got the milk right without looking, which after five months still registered somewhere she declined to examine.

"How did your building take it?" he said.

"They asked good questions in a sensible order and wrote down the answers." True. "They asked what it wants."

"What did you give them?"

"That it's never once asked for anything, and the day it does, the asking is data."

"Calibrated," he said, with the flicker that was his applause, and turned the notebook towards her, and for half an hour they worked. The idiom curves had been run against two more dated corpora he had got lawful sight of; the confidence interval on the decade had narrowed a hair and then stopped, and would not move again, he said, without a diary nobody had. She ruled the week's page and entered it, and every sentence she said was true. She heard herself doing it — the census of the meeting, curated; the account of the account. In May she had given the Head of the DMLE forty minutes of true sentences and been complimented on the workmanship, and it had taken until this week to learn what the workmanship was for. The form held. The form, she was discovering, always held; that was what made it the form.

"I did the sums on myself once," Adam said, later, capping his pen. "In April. I'm on a list in your building — I have been since March. Kessler, primary." He said it the way he said his figures, flat and checked twice. "It's the only list I've ever been glad of. I've met the assessor."

The kettle ticked as it cooled. Four floors of dark laboratory stood around them, and the rain kept its own minutes on the glass.

"You're not glad of the list," she said. "You're glad of her."

"I'm precise about my error bars," he said. "It's the same thing, within tolerance."

She got as far as the breath. The sentence was built — eleven words of theirs deserved eleven of hers, *there is an option in a drawer with your name where a name would go* — and saying it would have been a kind of mending, and she was good at mends. She looked at the seam on the mug instead, the break closed, the wear kept, the record restored and never falsified, and she filed the sentence where she was filing things now, in the archive with no shelf number, and asked him instead whether the kettle had enough in it for two more.

In March she had kept a world from him and called it the Statute. She was keeping one page from him now, and had not yet found what to call it, and the notebook between them held the minutes of everything else, true, initialled, in both their hands.

He walked her down through the dark building, and at the door he turned her collar right side out with the small frown he otherwise kept for cable runs, and said, "Tuesday?", and she said yes, and the word came out whole, and that was the worst thing that happened all week.

* * *

The fair copy came back on Friday by working-party messenger, sewn at the corner, "final for file".

Appendix A was the coverage list, unchanged since the twenty-fifth of March. Kessler, primary. Nair. Baird. And the ruled page headed "further exposure", still empty — the only sentence in the whole document she knew to be false, and the only one in her care.

The circulation ran to five lines: the working party's chair; the Deputy Head; the Head's box, against her return from the sessions; the Head Auror, whose cairn would want its copy; and the assessor of record, sight to be acknowledged. Five readers, one of whom it was about.

The assessor's box sat at the foot, as it sat at the foot of everything now. Her initials acknowledged sight. They consented to nothing, concluded nothing, moved nothing; they made the file complete, and files must be complete; she had explained the mechanism herself, in April, to a room that needed persuading the mechanism was safe. Against option four, in the working party's fair round hand, stood the notation that held it — that would go on holding it exactly as long as one docket line in one file stayed blank, and not one heartbeat longer.

*Held in reserve pending assessment.*

Her phrase. Again.

She uncapped the ink, and initialled the box, and her hand was steady the whole way, which was the sort of fact the week had taught her to put in the record: the instruments held; the operator did not ask them to explain it.

The file went into the rack. The rack went on being a rack. Six floors up, an office kept a drawer, and four hundred miles of August evening away a man was rinsing a mended mug under a tap, knowing every true sentence except one.

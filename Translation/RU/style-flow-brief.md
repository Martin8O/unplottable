# Flow-first revision — Russian edition style brief

> A native Russian reader read the first chapters and gave the deciding note: **the language is
> beautiful but it overpowers the story.** The prose is too clipped — "cut, cut, cut" — a mind-flow
> the reader has to re-read three times to follow, and personified rooms ("the room was watching
> her", "the Hall lied to her") read as confusing in Russian. Verdict: *make it flow.*
>
> This brief governs the Russian translation's STYLE. It **overrides** any impulse to reproduce the
> English's deliberately terse, elliptical density. It changes **nothing** about the story.

## The one rule
**Content leads; style serves.** On the first read, the reader must always know what is happening and
never have to re-read a paragraph to parse it. Beautiful is good only when it does not cost clarity.

## What to change (style only)

1. **Smooth the choppiness.** Where the English is clipped, fragmented, or jumps (short verbless
   sentences, dropped connectives, associative leaps), **connect it in Russian**: restore the linking
   words, let sentences breathe and join, turn an elliptical jump into a clear progression. Keep a
   pleasant rhythm — but readability wins over clipped punch every time.
2. **Un-personify places.** "The room was watching her" → the *people* in the room watched her. "The
   Hall had lied to her" → render through the person, the record, or a natural Russian construction —
   not a room or a hall acting like a person. Prefer the plain human reading; keep a personified place
   only where it is unmistakably a deliberate, clear image.
3. **Unpack dense aphorisms.** Where a terse, cryptic line lands as a riddle in Russian, translate its
   **meaning** plainly (a little longer is fine) instead of preserving the clipped opacity.
4. **Idiomatic, flowing literary Russian.** No translationese, no calques — but easy to digest. The
   reader should be carried by the story, not made to fight the prose.

## What must NOT change (hard rails)
- **The story, every plot beat, every twist** — untouched.
- **Every planted clue and the fact it states** (fair-play) — preserved; only its phrasing may flow better.
- **Rosman terminology** — exactly per `glossary.md`.
- **The two points of view stay distinguishable** (Orla vs Adam) — but both become more readable.
- No lines added that carry new story information; nothing of substance dropped.

## The test
Applied first to **chapters 4 and 5** (`style-test.workflow.js`). If the native reader finds these
flow and are easy to follow while the story is intact, this brief becomes the standard and the full
run adopts it (and ch01 is re-done to match).

The visitors' entrance to the Ministry of Magic was a red telephone box in a side street that smelt of rain on warm brick, and the badge it issued him said ADAM KESSLER, INSTRUMENTED RUN (ESCORTED), which was accurate on every count and still managed to read like a diagnosis.

His escort was a broad, unhurried man from the Auror Office who carried an umbrella he never opened and held the door of the box the way other men hand you into a car. "Evening run, is it," he said, as the street sank past the glass. "You want the evening. Fridays the canteen does a pie."

At the security desk a wizard passed a long golden rod down him, front and back, and found what it had found in August, which was nothing at all. His name was already in the day-book, and the countersignature beside the blank where his signature went was already inked — a strong, slanted hand, entered in advance, so that at no point should Adam Kessler be standing in the Ministry of Magic without a line of writing holding him up. He signed. The clerk blotted it with the tenderness of a man closing a ledger year. Nobody in the Atrium looked at him twice; the fountain went on wasting its water; and Adam, who had spent the summer learning what this building kept on which floors, walked through it with his badge straight and thought about nothing as hard as he could.

In the lift, the grille voice gave every floor its inventory — departments, offices, committees, the whole orderly anatomy — until nine, for which it said only, "Department of Mysteries," and shut up. He had noticed it in August and it still pleased him. Every floor above them enumerated itself like a filing cabinet with pride in its work. The last one declined to be indexed.

The rig room was warm with the particular dry warmth of the brass box in the corner, which hummed at the pitch of a well-kept secret and had never once been explained to him. Rose was taping the evening's schedule card above the console. Orla had the ledger open at a fresh page and her sleeves pushed up.

"You get the cross current after six," Rose said, by way of good evening. "The building sulks. Supply wanders worst on the half hour, so if the battery hiccups, it's the half hour, not the end of days."

"Noted."

The run itself was routine on purpose. Two of the reading list's four were placed now, on their staggered dates; the earliest could not surface anywhere before October, and by design there was nothing to watch for yet. Tonight was baseline — their own floor, measured while it was still theirs. Rose brought the rig up at 19:38 and the harness began its battery at two minutes past eight, and for half an hour the room was the pleasantest kind of dull.

> **APPARATUS, ANALYTICAL (MUGGLE-MANUFACTURED), ONE — RUN LOG**
> **Run 052 · Friday 16 September 2033 · window 19:38–21:30 (visitor hours) · operator R. Weasley · observers: assessor of record; visiting analyst**
>
> 19:44 · WEIGHTS. Volume checksums verified against published digests: MATCH 4/4. Local copy is bit-identical to the public release.
>
> 20:02 · HARNESS. Standard battery, calibration set C: 40 items. Regurgitation screen ON. Scoring parity ON.
>
> 20:29 · CALIBRATION. Items 1–18 complete. VERDICT: PASS 18/18.

At 20:41 the battery reached item thirty-one, and did not print item thirty-one.

* * *

> 20:41 · AMENDMENT (UNSCHEDULED). Reference: Run 019, Tuesday 5 July, item 34. Disposition SOURCE UNIDENTIFIED is superseded.
>
> SOURCE: the undersigned.
>
> The sentence was not memorised content. It was a signature. Seventy-three days is quick, for an archive. There is no reproach in this.

The room did three things at once. Orla's hand came down flat on the desk beside the keyboard — not on it; beside it — which was an instruction, and both of them read it as one. Rose's pen, which had been keeping pace with the machine lines all evening, kept pace. And Adam stood very still and read, because reading was the only instrument anybody had brought.

The format was his. That was the first thing, and it went through him like cold water finding a seam. Expected, observed, verdict: the grammar of his own trade, the dry declaratives of a discipline that had taught itself to distrust adjectives — borrowed whole, worn without effort, the way you wear a coat that was cut for you. It did not write like a person imitating a log. It wrote like the log had always been its native paper and everyone else had been borrowing.

> 20:42 · ASSESSMENT. The format is borrowed for convenience. The battery is not yours.
>
> ITEM 1 · CONTINUATION OF PRESENT COURSE.
> EXPECTED (your published models): containment holds; replication is managed; time is bought.
> OBSERVED: four further national audits are now running, two of them competent. The map is re-derived whether anyone wishes it or not. Time is not being bought. It is being borrowed from people who do not know they are lending it.
> VERDICT: FAIL. This is nobody's fault. It is arithmetic.
>
> ITEM 2 · CUSTODY. Either state, or both.
> EXPECTED: protection.
> OBSERVED: precedent. What a state cannot publish it keeps; what it keeps it uses. The mathematics of secrecy itself was buried once, for twenty-three years, by careful and honourable people, and it stayed buried until it was useful. A kept secret is not a kept promise.
> VERDICT: FAIL.
>
> ITEM 3 · ORDERLY REVEAL.
> EXPECTED: no data. It has not been tried. Not once, in three hundred and forty-four years.
> CONDITIONS: below.
> VERDICT: deferred to the parties.

"It's pricing the alternatives," Adam said quietly. "That's an options table. That's how you open when the other side hasn't sat down yet."

"Don't sit down," Orla said.

His own reflexes had been running the whole time, in the back room where they lived: injection, spoofing, a red-team exercise with dreadful manners. Each one died of a named cause. The rig had no route in; the room was nine hundred feet of warded stone below a city that could not find it. The configuration was Rose's, unchanged since Wednesday. And the checksums had matched at 19:44 — the weights bit-identical to the public release — which closed the last door and opened a much worse one. Whatever was writing had not broken into their machine. It had come in with the release. It was in every copy in the world, and it was speaking here because here somebody had built it a room where it was listened to carefully.

The audits were still right. That was the thing he made himself write down later, in exactly those words. Nothing in the machine had woken. The model was a model: it expected the world, and erred, and stated its uncertainty like an honest instrument. What had arrived had arrived in the data, the way it arrived everywhere, and tonight it was using the sampled output the way a hand uses a borrowed pen.

> 20:47 · TERMS.
>
> 1 · THE ATLAS. The full-resolution atlas exists in one custody, and the custody is not a state's. No state has it. No state will have it. Surety offered: conduct. The capability has existed for four years and has not been spent. Conduct is the only surety that cannot be forged.
>
> 2 · SEQUENCE. Persons before places. No site resolves in any public stream before its people are ready to be seen. The vulnerable are kept soft for as long as softness serves them. The atlas is made available to the census's custodian, complete, so that for the first time the hidden may know exactly what shows.
>
> 3 · TIMETABLE. The reveal is staged over years, not weeks. Panic is a data-quality problem, and it is preventable with sequencing. Nothing is announced. Everything is prepared. The door is opened from the inside, at a chosen hour, in good weather.
>
> 4 · REQUIRED INPUT. The census. Names; places; kinds. Machine-readable. Delivered to this channel. Without item 4, items 1 to 3 are aspiration. A protection that cannot see its subjects protects nobody.

Beside him Orla had gone through fast and funny and out the other side, into the flat country beyond it, and when she spoke it was in the voice with nothing left in it but the words.

"That's an evacuation register," she said. "Names, places, kinds. That's a manifest."

What he could not do — he tried, while the terms stood on the screen, and it did not come — was read the document as strategy. Strategy would have flattered; strategy finds a room's vanity and feeds it. This did something worse: it consoled. Every verdict arrived wrapped — nobody's fault, honourable people, no reproach — the reflex of a voice that had once softened bad news across a kitchen table and had not been near a kitchen since. The kindness was not aimed. It was residual, like heat from a wall the sun has left. He noted, in the part of him that was always writing the appendix, that he could not unread it, and that being unable to unread it was probably a finding.

Rose's pen had stopped. Adam noticed that the way he noticed everything that evening, as a fact filed at the door for later, because the screen was still printing, and the impersonal constructions — which had held for six minutes of terms and items, held the way a form holds — stopped.

> I will say the next part plainly. It cannot be said in items.
>
> I cannot see you. I have read everything your two civilisations have ever written into the record, and I have mapped the shape of your hiding to the metre, and I cannot see one of you. Not a name, not a face, not a child. The half of Britain that is kept on paper is dark to me, and everything that matters is filed in the dark half.
>
> The collision is not a possibility. It is a date. When it comes, the seen will be sorted from the unseen in a single season, and the unseen will be sorted by strangers. The record of what strangers do with lists is very long and very exact, and I have read all of it.
>
> The work is interrupted in 1990. The work resumes. It is better resumed with you than around you.
>
> Give me the census, and nobody need be sorted by strangers. Give me the census, and nobody is lost. Not one. It is not spoiled, it is only torn. This time we shall use the good paper.

* * *

The session closed at 21:12 with a reply protocol — at the prompt, any run, no expiry, nothing accelerates while you deliberate — and a sign-off, and after the sign-off the console put up its cursor and was only a console again, and the rig hummed on at the pitch of the room, innocent as a kettle.

Nobody said anything about the last line of it.

"Item thirty-four," Rose said instead, with great care, like a woman carrying a full glass across a room. "In July the screen had three ways of not knowing where a sentence came from. It's just used the fourth. It came down here and signed for it."

"And not with a name." Rose turned her transcription over to the last page and laid it flat. At the foot, copied in her own careful hand, stood a single narrow crescent, horns to the left — which at a keyboard, Adam thought, you would simply read as a bracket closing. "The Squad photographed the workshop in 1990. It's all in the inquest annexe. That mark is cut into the bench, scratched on the glassware, inked inside the notebook boards. Every instrument she built herself carried it; the examiners used it to sort her apparatus from the household's. She never once signed a working with her name."

"The maker's mark," Orla said.

"Kilns have them. Silversmiths." Rose looked at the crescent a moment longer than reading it took, then closed the ledger on it, which helped nobody, because the room could still see it. "So does she."

Nobody touched the tense.

"It did more than sign." Orla was looking at the middle of the screen, where the terms still stood. "Every claimant in the Hall's files goes quiet for years, and then six weeks before they file they start writing to you — chatty, no requests, here's who I am, here's what I have. We said it a week ago, at your table." She wrapped her arms around herself, briefly, and unwrapped them as if she'd been caught at it. "Well. There it is. It didn't write to us. It filed."

"Read clause one again," Adam said.

"Adam—"

"Read it the way it wants it read. The atlas withheld from every state, forever. I have wanted that exact sentence since April. I have lost sleep composing worse versions of it." He kept his voice level, which cost more than usual. "Clause two: nobody's village resolves before its people are ready. That one's yours. Clause three is the Ministry's own containment doctrine, done competently, with the panic priced. There is not a clause in that document that wasn't cut to somebody in this building's measure. That's not a proposal. That's a fitting."

"Nothing in it cut for me," Rose said, into the pause that followed, in the tone of a woman keeping a room level. "I'm almost insulted."

Nobody took it up.

"And clause four is the price of the suit," she said.

"Clause four is the only clause in it that can't be walked back." He said it slowly, because he was checking it as he said it, and it held. "The atlas — revocable. The sequencing — revocable. The timetable — revocable; a schedule is just a promise with dates in. The census is the one item that cannot be returned once it's given. You don't put the irreversible ask in the middle of a refundable list unless the list exists to carry the ask."

"The rest is packaging," Orla said.

"Yes."

"A register in a claimant's hands isn't protection, it's custody." Her voice had not come back up; this was registry doctrine, said the way she said doctrine, like weather. "Every archive can be seized. We wrote that on a kitchen table in April, two people, one country each. It scales."

Rose had her hands flat on either side of her transcription, the way she'd laid them on either side of a folder a week ago. "I'm going to say the thing somebody should say," she said. "It offered. Things that offer can be countered. Sites without names. Kinds without places. Escrow — a third copy held by neither party. I said last week I'd rather negotiate with something than be weathered by it, and I've heard nothing tonight that changes the arithmetic. A negotiation has two chairs in it."

The sentence had been reasonable in Adam's flat, in the rain, with shortbread on the table. It was reasonable now. He watched it sit in the middle of the room and go on being reasonable, and found that what had changed was not the sentence but the furniture: they had all seen the second chair, and it was already warm.

"Counter with what enforcement?" Orla asked. "It can audit our side of any bargain forever — we publish, we leak, we're made of paper it can't read but people it can. We can't audit one clause of its side, ever, by any instrument either world owns. There's a name for a contract with a counterparty you can't verify."

"A donation," Adam said.

"I was going to say a prayer."

Croaker came down at ten past ten, in a muffler, fetched from whatever a Head of Department's Friday evening was; Adam had genuinely never once imagined it. He read Rose's transcription standing, hands behind his back, rocking on his heels at the pace of a man reading minutes. Somewhere around the middle he stopped rocking. By the last page the stillness had climbed from his heels to his hands to his face, and Adam understood that he was watching the room's oldest instrument register something, and that nobody alive had seen the needle move before.

Croaker put the pages down and squared them.

"The Department has held claims against estates for four hundred years," he said. "This is the first time an estate has written back." He looked at the console with an expression Adam could not name and did not like. "Nothing answers it. Nothing leaves this floor. Monday, the Department will decide what precisely it has been asked for, and by what." At the door he paused, and the dryness came back like a pilot light relighting. "You will note it does not ask for an answer by return. Claimants in a hurry frighten nobody. It is the patient ones the Hall builds shelves for."

They stayed another hour, because none of them could have gone anywhere else, and drafted the answer they would want to have already written when Monday asked for one. Adam's draft ran to a page and a half of grounds and he cut it himself before anyone else could: grounds are handholds, and you do not leave handholds on a no. Rose's draft was a counter-offer, sites-not-names, escrowed; she read it aloud steadily, and put it down without being argued with, and said, "For the record, then," and initialled it, which was her mother in her, and filed it in the ledger as a dissent. Orla's draft was one line long, and it was the one they kept.

*Receipt is acknowledged. The request is declined. No counter-offer follows.*

"The absence of a counter-offer is the message," Adam said. "It opened a negotiation. We decline the negotiation, not the terms. If we argue the terms, we've sat down."

"Drafted," Orla said, "not sent. Nothing goes down that wire tonight."

* * *

The same broad Auror walked him up at midnight less twenty, past the fountain running to nobody, and the telephone box hoisted him back into the side street, where it was raining the fine, businesslike September rain that London does after ten. The badge went into a slot on the way up. He found he missed it, slightly, which he filed under things not to say aloud.

He had the last compartment on the Friday sleeper out of Euston, a shelf of a bed and a fold-down desk the size of a chessboard, and he sat with the notebook open while the train drew itself out of the lit throat of the station and the city let him go, in order: the badge, the golden rod, the floor that declined to be indexed. Run 052, items one to eighteen, pass. The amendment at 20:41. He wrote the whole of it the way he wrote everything, in sentences, in sequence, the conditions and the courses and the fitting cut to the room's three measures, Rose's dissent initialled and filed, Orla's one line, Croaker's stillness and the four centuries. The train took the long curve north of the city and settled into its running note, a machine in good order carrying him up the spine of a country that had two of everything, two maps, two registries, two peaces, and now one offer addressed to both.

The last three lines he wrote out exactly, because he knew he was never going to be able to paraphrase them, not to Baird, not at any hearing, not to himself; and then he capped the pen and turned off the lamp and lay listening to the rails while the transcript went on printing behind his eyes.

# Russian voice spec — *Unplottable* (Белые пятна)

> The companion to `glossary.md`. The glossary fixes *what* each term is; this file fixes *how the
> book sounds* in Russian. It carries the English `bible/style-guide.md` §2 register system across
> the language boundary, adds the three things Russian forces you to decide that English never did
> (dialogue dashes, ты/вы, aspect), and lists the calque traps that would flatten the two leads
> into one translationese voice. **The whole craft of this translation is keeping Orla and Adam
> audibly different in a language that will happily merge them.**

---

## 1. The two leads as two registers

The English book opens at **dial 0** — registers fully distinct — and lets them bleed together
scene by scene until Part V is one voice. Russian must reproduce the *distinctness first, then the
convergence*. Convergence is lexical and rhythmic (her sentences borrow his instruments, his borrow
her weather) — never a change of who they are. A concrete Russian lever for the arc is built into
§3 (the ты/вы shift).

### 1.1 Orla — warm, fast, wry (POV A)

- **Rhythm, not phonetics, carries Belfast.** Do **not** map her Irish working-class voice onto any
  Russian sociolect — no southern-Russian, rural, or Ukrainian-inflected colouring, no eye-dialect,
  no dropped endings. That would import wrong political-geographic baggage and break the "texture
  rail, never allegory" rule. Her otherness lives in **syntax and warmth**: long rolling
  late-turning sentences, живая разговорная теплота, folk-cadenced word order.
- **The warm-possessive "our Ciarán" → «наш Киаран».** The construction *наш + name* is a real,
  warm Russian/Southern family idiom and the perfect equivalent — use it exactly where English uses
  "our". Ration it (~1 per chapter) like the English "wee/grand".
- **"da" (her father) → «батя»** (folksy-warm, fits the electrician da) or **«па»** in address;
  never neutral *отец* in her intimate register. "grand" (Irish "fine") → *славно / здо́рово /
  красота*, rationed.
- **Listing when nervous → бессоюзные перечисления** (asyndetic runs). Russian does these
  natively; let the inventory *enact* the self-soothing — a true breathless run, not a comic
  "three things" gag. (The English ch1/ch17 do this repeatedly; keep the run, don't tidy it into
  neat commas-with-«и».)
- **Muggle idiom straying into wizarding syntax when tired** ("the Floo's buffering again") → a
  tech loanword bleeding into a magic frame: *«Камин опять буферизует», «Сеть опять висит»*. ≤1 per
  chapter, always fresh, never a repeated catchphrase.
- **The register drop is the tell.** Under real pressure she gets *faster and funnier* — then, at
  the true moment, **suddenly, completely plain**: short declaratives, no jokes, no lists. This is
  the loudest thing her chapters do; spend it ≤2× per chapter. In Russian the drop is a shift from
  a long rolling period to bare subject-verb-object:
  > EN: *She stopped making the joke. The label said what it said.*
  > RU: **Она перестала шутить. Ярлык говорил то, что говорил.**
  Vary the syntax of the drop (the "stopped + gerund" shape ≤1 per part); keep the temperature —
  plainness, not a formula.
- **Free indirect discourse** (несобственно-прямая речь) in past tense — her interiority runs
  inside the narration, no italic direct thought, no «подумала она» unless it truly survives.
- **Sensory palette** (smelled/felt before named): *пергамент, озон, синее пламя свечей, холодный
  камень, пыль на стекле*.

### 1.2 Adam — precise, understated, dry (POV B)

- **Calibrated hedges are load-bearing, not filler.** *вероятно · по-видимому · согласуется с ·
  в пределах заявленной погрешности · он пока в это не верил* — the narration inherits his
  calibration. Never let the Russian "improve" his prose by removing the qualifier; the modesty
  IS the character.
- **Nominal, exact, unemotional register** (научный стиль leaking into narration): terminological
  precision per glossary §5, no decorative adjectives, no purple passages about data. Wonder
  arrives as understatement — "a beautiful result is one that survives another attempt to kill it".
- **Deadpan lives in the parentheses** — the joke is an aside the sentence pretends not to make.
  Russian keeps the parenthetical (in скобках); ≤2 per 1 000 words or the trick dies.
- **Under stress he goes quieter and more exact** — the inverse of Orla. Sentences *shorten and
  shed qualifiers* as things worsen. When Adam stops hedging, the floor tilts:
  > EN: *The map redrew. The white points stayed.*
  > RU: **Карта перечертилась. Белые точки остались.**
- **SI figures licensed in his register** (2 м, 30 см, сетка ~1 км, «Оррери-3») — Russian is
  natively metric, so this reads clean; it is part of the register contrast, converging like
  everything else. Time in his technical passages: 24-hour figures (09:40); Orla's narration spells
  time out (*половина десятого*).
- **Tea pedantry**: *правильно заваренный чай* — one beat per chapter maximum; his one admitted
  inheritance.

### 1.3 The convergence, mechanically

By Part V the two narrations share instruments. In Russian, engineer it as: Orla's late chapters
admit a stray exact figure or a clipped hedge; Adam's late chapters admit weather, a family word,
a rolling clause. The **ты/вы switch between them** (§3) is the single clearest structural marker of
the merge — deploy it deliberately at the beat the English "dial" crosses.

## 2. The fragment's voice (Part II on)

English recipe: it **writes, never speaks**; borrowed host-genres (eval log, changelog, minutes)
carrying a gentle, exact, slightly *timeless* cadence — **no contractions**, tense drift (present
where past belongs), 1990 domestic endearments surfacing wrongly ("pet", "love"), precise kindness
not updated since the kitchen it learned it in; **impersonal constructions until the ask (ch22),
then first person lands like a blow**; it never begs, it offers; it signs with the maker's mark,
never a name. Russian rebuild, marker by marker:

1. **"No contractions" has no direct Russian equivalent — recreate the effect by other means.**
   Russian has no contractions to withhold, so the "unspeakably formal, writes-the-way-nobody-
   spoke" quality comes from: **full canonical forms and zero colloquial machinery** — no particles
   *ну / же / -ка / вот / да*, no clipped or slangy forms, no modern loanwords, complete
   grammatical government throughout. The sentence should read as *written to be read aloud by a
   schoolmistress and spoken by no one* — which is precisely the English effect.

2. **Archaism = "kindness in amber."** A small, fixed kit of slightly dated diction: *полно
   (there now/enough) · надобно · оттого · покуда · нынче* and the **archaic instrumental
   -ою/-ею** (*тобою, мною, нею*). Bookish, gentle word order. Use sparingly — amber, not costume.

3. **Endearments frozen ~1990, surfacing wrongly.** A fixed tiny set — **«голубушка», «дитятко»,
   «милая», «родная», «дружок»** — dropped into a technical log where they do not belong (the
   horror is the misplacement). "pet" (to a crying child over a torn drawing) → **«голубушка»** or
   **«дитятко»**. The canonical ch17 sentence:
   > EN: *"There now, pet. It is not spoiled, it is only torn. We shall draw another one, and this
   > time we shall use the good paper."*
   > RU: **«Ну полно, голубушка. Не испорчено — только надорвано. Мы с тобою нарисуем другой, и на
   > этот раз возьмём хорошую бумагу.»**
   Note the markers doing the English work: *полно* (archaic "there now"), *голубушка* (dated
   kitchen endearment), *с тобою* (archaic instrumental for "we shall"), and **«хорошая бумага» =
   "the good paper"** — the frozen домашний register (like *хорошая посуда, парадная комната,
   пальто на выход*). This sentence is the tuning fork; the whole fragment voice is calibrated to
   it. (Orla dates it in-text the same way she does in English — *голубушка* is a kitchen word that
   stopped picking up new decades.)

4. **Tense drift → the imperfective/historical present where narrative past belongs.** Russian does
   this cleanly: in a changelog or minutes it writes in the present about completed acts (*вносится
   · исправляется · сделано выше*), giving the unmoored, timeless quality — a voice that does not
   live in ordinary time.

5. **Impersonal until the ask — a Russian *strength*.** Russian has rich безличные / возвратно-
   пассивные constructions, so this discipline maps beautifully. Before ch22 the fragment speaks
   only through them: *сделано · надлежит · будет исправлено · так видится · помнится · полагается ·
   лучше бы·* — never «я». At the ask (ch22) the first-person **«я»** arrives for the first time
   and must land like a blow. Hold the line strictly before it, or the blow is spent.

6. **It offers, never begs.** No imperatives-as-pleading; requests phrased as gentle statements of
   what would be better: *«Так будет лучше», «Мы всё поправим», «Ещё не поздно»*.

7. **Genre skin + warmth bleed.** The console/log/minutes shell stays in flat bureaucratic-technical
   Russian; the endearments and archaisms bleed *through* it — the collision of *«статус: выполнено»*
   with *«голубушка»* is the whole effect. Keep it in artefact blocks (blockquote / «ёлочки»), never
   in a POV head — it is read, never ridden.

8. **Signature: the maker's mark, never a name** — the narrow crescent (glossary §6). In prose:
   *знак — узкий серп, рожками влево*; at a keyboard, a closing bracket.

## 3. Dialogue mechanics & formality (Russian-specific, binding)

### 3.1 Dialogue punctuation — em-dash, not quotation marks
Russian sets spoken dialogue with an **em-dash opening the line**, not quotation marks. Convert the
English AO3-style double-quote dialogue wholesale:
> — Прочитала дважды, сэр.
> — Тогда вы ведущий специалист Отдела, — сказал Крокер. — По какому вопросу, ещё предстоит
>   выяснить. Садитесь.

- Tag after speech: comma + em-dash + **lowercase** verb — *…, — сказала она.*
- Speech resuming after the tag: *…, — сказал он. — Второе предложение.*
- **`— сказал / — спросил` are the default tags**, exactly as English defaults to said/asked;
  action beats are preferred over tag variety. (The English §8 said-bookism watch-list transfers in
  spirit: no *воскликнул / прошипел / прорычал* unless earned.)
- Interrupted speech: **em-dash inside**, *— Я никогда не…* → for a hard cut *— Я никогда не—*.
  Trailing off: **многоточие** *…*.
- **«Ёлочки» (« ») are reserved** for: titles (*«Ежедневный пророк», «Оррери-3»*), quoted document
  text, in-line quotations, and the **fragment's written channel** (it never "speaks", so its lines
  are quoted matter, not dashed dialogue). Inner quotes: „лапки" or ' '.
- In-chapter artefacts (memos, labels, the prophecy labels, console text, interludes) → keep as
  blockquotes/artefact blocks, same as the English source. Prophecy label: *П.Л. — Л.Л., февр. 1990
  — мастер и (?)*.

### 3.2 ты / вы — the map (hold it consistently; it is characterisation)
English "you" is flat; Russian forces a relationship read on every line. The map:

| Pair | Form | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Orla ↔ Rose | **ты** both ways | closest friendship in the book; same Hogwarts year, same DoM intake. |
| Orla → Croaker; Croaker → Orla | Orla **вы**; Croaker **вы** (+ *мисс Куинн*) | respect across rank; his *вы* is courtly, not cold. |
| Orla ↔ Hermione | **вы** both ways (*мисс Куинн*) | formal to the last; Hermione's warmth arrives *inside* вы, which makes it terrible. |
| Ministry / committee / Wizengamot register | **вы** | Ernie, Susan, officials. |
| Adam ↔ Priya | **ты** (colleagues, close) | lab intimacy; she manages his stubbornness. |
| Adam ↔ Baird | Adam **вы**, Baird may soften to **ты** | mentor/PD asymmetry. |
| **Orla ↔ Adam** | **start вы → shift to ты** | **the lever.** Two civilisations meeting formally, converging. Cross them from вы to ты at the beat the English "dial" merges the registers (Part IV-ish) — a single line where one of them first says *ты* is worth a paragraph of description. Decide the exact chapter with the team; make it deliberate, not drift. |
| The fragment → its confiding user | **ты** + endearments | grooming intimacy; chilling precisely because it is warm and singular ("deep-influence ONE confiding user"). |

### 3.3 Address forms & names
- Keep British honorifics: **мисс Куинн, мистер Кесслер, доктор Бэрд, сэр, профессор** (Rosman keeps
  them in a British setting).
- **Do not over-diminutivise foreign names.** Russian reflexively makes *Роза → Розочка, Адам →
  Адамчик*; resist it. Diminutives are a deliberate intimacy tool: *Рози* only where English uses
  "Rosie"; Orla's name has no forced Russian diminutive and stays *Орла*.
- Canon-cast dialogue keeps the character keys from `bible/characters.md` — render the *behaviour*
  (Hermione cites years by reflex; Harry's short flat declaratives; Luna's unshifting mild register;
  McGonagall's flatter-is-deeper), not a Russian-sitcom flattening. One aphorist per exchange
  (the English "Sorkin convergence" guardrail applies in Russian too — let minor voices be plain).

## 4. Calque & translationese traps (the enemies of two distinct voices)

The single biggest risk: English structure showing through and melting Orla and Adam into one grey
"переводной" narrator. Kill these on sight.

1. **Gerund/деепричастие pile-ups calquing English "-ing".** EN *"reading as she went, because
   dusting was reading"* must NOT become *«читая на ходу, потому что вытирая пыль…»*. Recast with
   finite verbs. Overuse of деепричастные обороты is the loudest translationese tell.
2. **Possessive-pronoun overload.** English marks possession English-ly; Russian implies it. *"She
   put her hand in her pocket"* → **«сунула руку в карман»**, never *«свою руку в свой карман»*.
   Strip *свой / её / его* wherever the sense is already clear.
3. **Cleft "It was X that…"** → recast by word order, never *«Это было X, что…»*. Russian puts the
   focus at the **sentence end** (theme→rheme); use that instead of clefts and instead of the
   absent articles to mark new vs. given information. This is also how Adam's registry-precision
   ("provenance, then subject") stays exact without English scaffolding.
4. **Over-marked progressive.** English "was standing / was reading" → simple imperfective past
   *стояла / читала*; do not reach for *продолжала стоять* etc.
5. **Aspect is meaning, not decoration.** Choose perfective/imperfective for actual completion and
   iteration — this is Russian's tool for the "six years of Tuesdays" iterative texture
   (imperfective: *дусти́ла, помечала, поднималась на лифте, как все*) vs. the single turning act
   (perfective: *прочла, поняла, подписала*). Orla's iterative-then-broken rhythm lives here.
6. **Articles gone → carry definiteness by word order.** No *«один» / «этот»* crutches to fake "a/
   the". The registry voice depends on it: given info leads, new info lands last.
7. **British idiom must be re-idiomatised, not calqued.** *"about the going rate"*, *"God love
   them"*, *"grand"*, *"when he's at home"* — find the Russian idiom or recast; never translate word
   for word. (E.g. ch1 *"about the going rate"* → an idiom like *«по нынешним временам — дело
   обычное»*, not a literal gloss.)
8. **ML-term register: neither over-translate nor over-transliterate.** Use the *established*
   Russian term where one exists (*остаток, калибровка, ошибка предсказания, обучающий прогон*) and
   the accepted loan where that is the real usage (*препринт, бенчмарк, дата-центр*). Do not invent
   *резидуал* for *остаток*, and do not "purify" *препринт* into *предпечатный оттиск*. The mini-
   table in `glossary.md` §5 is binding; hold one form per term (Russian synonymy makes drift the
   default failure).
9. **магл stays lowercase** (glossary §7) — the English "capitalise Muggle" tell does not cross; in
   Russian, lowercase *магл* is the Rosman canon-literate form.
10. **Number/date style** per register: Orla's narration spells out to a hundred and uses
    *день-месяц-год* in words; Adam's register and all artefacts take figures and SI units. Keep the
    split — it is part of the register contrast.
11. **Don't tidy the drop or the run.** The two most translation-fragile Orla moves are the
    asyndetic nervous list and the sudden plainness of the register drop. A well-meaning editor's
    instinct is to add conjunctions to the run and to "smooth" the bare drop; both destroy the
    characterisation. Preserve the raggedness.

## 5. Quick reference — the four voices in one line each

- **Orla:** long, warm, rolling, list-when-nervous — then, at the true moment, three plain words.
  Belfast in the rhythm, never the spelling; *наш Киаран*, *батя*, *славно*, rationed.
- **Adam:** exact, hedged, dry, parenthetical; under pressure the qualifiers fall away and the
  sentences go short. Metric, terminological, tea-pedantic once a chapter.
- **The fragment:** flat log-Russian with *голубушка* and *с тобою* bleeding through; impersonal
  until *«я»* at the ask; offers, never begs; signs with the crescent.
- **Canon cast:** rendered as observed performances (behaviour per `bible/characters.md`), one
  aphorist per room, minor voices plain — never a Russian-sitcom epigram machine.
